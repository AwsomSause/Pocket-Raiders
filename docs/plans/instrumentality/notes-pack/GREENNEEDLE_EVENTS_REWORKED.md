# GREENNEEDLE — YOUR EVENTS, REWORKED

**Extends:** your `Greenneedle.md` (62 events) and the notes in `greenneedle_extras.md`.
**What this is:** every event of yours, kept, with the notes turned into things a builder can implement: the beats in order, each ending, the numbers, and what comes back later. Where your draft left a balance question open (Mega Pidgeot, North's Wind, Haunted, the Network Machine, the belt Primeape), this document closes it.
*All numbers are starting values for tuning. Abilities and moves named here are yours unless marked **(new)**.*

---

## 0. Rules that apply to every event

1. **Nothing here ends a run.** A lost event battle costs the event's reward, restarts a node, or changes the ending. The one event that touches the game-over screen (Flight North) *rescues* a run.
2. **No healing items in battle** (your rule in `Stuff.md`). No event reward below is an in-battle healing item. Revival is scarce, so the two effects here that revive (North's Wind, the Bug-type's return) are tightly limited.
3. **Ability budget.** An event ability may do one strong thing with a limit, or two small things. Stat boosts from one ability are capped at **+4 stages per battle**. Anything that triggers "on entry" triggers once per entry.
4. **Signature-move budget.** Power 60–90. One secondary effect. Any effect stronger than a normal move's (a sure flinch, a revive, a new status) has a per-battle limit.
5. **One Mega per battle**, for the player and for every opponent, however the Mega was unlocked.
6. **A Pokémon may leave.** Where an ending lets a Pokémon go, that ending always pays something else: a later return, an ally in a hard fight, or a keepsake.
7. **Afterstories are cheap.** Most are one background sprite or four lines of text in a later stage. They are listed because they are what makes the event feel remembered.

## A. The character stories

### 1. Poli-Pond — The Little Pokémon That Could
*(Miniature Episode, Companion Story, Region-Wide Story)*

- **Cold open:** A Poliwag is shoved out of the reeds and lands at the player's feet. It hides behind their legs. Five heads rise out of the water.
- **Training (this stage):** The Poliwag joins for the stage. It will not attack in its first battle; it looks at the ground. At three rests the player teaches one lesson each: *aim* (accuracy), *footing* (it stops flinching), *standing its ground* (it stops trying to run). It fails each lesson once before it gets it.
- **What the player can find out:** One Poliwhirl hangs back and will not look at the Poliwag. Talking to it at the pond reveals the group started out guarding the pond from trainers, and got cruel by habit.
- **Rematch (start of next stage):** One on one against the lead bully. The Poliwag wins by using a lesson, not by level: each lesson learned cancels one of the bully's tricks.
- **Endings:**
  - **Keep:** it gains **Kindness**: when it switches out, the Pokémon coming in has +1 priority on its first move. Twice per battle.
  - **Release:** it founds a training pond. It returns once, during an evil-team climax or the Champion battle, as a Poliwrath with three Poliwhirl: they take the field for three turns as allies. Afterwards it asks to join, at the party's average level, with Kindness.
  - **Reconcile** (needs the guilty Poliwhirl found): the bullies become its training partners. The later return brings four Poliwhirl and lasts four turns.
  - **Exceptional** (all three lessons learned on the first retry): it evolves into Poliwhirl during the rematch.
- **Afterstory:** The return is never announced. The player is losing; a familiar cry comes from off-screen. It recognises the player before the player recognises it.

### 2. Pidgey's Resolve — The Rivalry of the Sky
*(Region-Wide Story, Rival Episode)*

- **Opening:** A Pidgey and a Spearow are fighting over a branch. The player picks a side and watches a CPU-versus-CPU battle.

| The player backed | Result | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pidgey | Pidgey wins | A Flying HM. The story ends here. |
| Spearow | Spearow loses | Nothing. |
| Pidgey | Pidgey loses | Pidgey vows to get stronger. The story begins. |
| Spearow | Spearow wins | The same. Pidgey remembers who the player backed. |

- **Through the region:** Pidgey appears once per stage. The player may **spar** with it (a battle; it learns from what beat it and carries an answer next time) or **boost** it (give an item; it keeps every gift to the end). Now and then it is found training alone and does not notice the player.
- **Fearow is a character.** It is not cruel. It thinks Pidgey is wasting its life chasing something it cannot beat, and it says so by winning. It also grows: one new move per stage.
- **Finale (before the Elite Four):** Pidgeot against Fearow, CPU-versus-CPU, on a cliff in high wind, with wild Pokémon watching. If Pidgeot drops below 35% HP the player is asked who to cheer for.
  - **Cheer Pidgeot:** it Mega Evolves from resolve alone. *"Pidgeot's resolve is unwavering!"*
  - **Cheer Fearow:** Pidgeot is furious: Attack +2.
- **Reward:** If Pidgeot wins and was cheered, it asks to join. Base ability **Scrappy**. It can Mega Evolve **without a Mega Stone**, so it holds a normal item. Mega ability **Sky's Resolve**: its Normal-type moves become Flying and gain 20%, and its moves cannot miss; moves used against it cannot miss either. That last half is the cost and should stay.
- **Other endings:** If Fearow wins after being cheered, it lands beside the player and may be recruited, with **Sniper** and *Drill Run*. If Pidgeot won after the player cheered Fearow, it leaves without looking back, and is met in the next region for one more fight.
- **Afterstory:** Whichever bird did not join is seen overhead at the start of the next region, alone, still training.

### 3. Viridian Forest — The Samurai's Challenge
*(Pseudo Stage, Rival Episode)*

- **Rule:** The first Bug-type caught in the forest is the player's duelist. It walks beside the player for the whole forest, and other Bug-types react to it: Caterpie crowd round a Caterpie; Weedle keep their distance from a Pinsir.
- **The Samurai** comments on its training at two points. His own Bug-types have habits: a Metapod that Hardens with a special flourish, a Pinsir that refuses to attack anything that is not looking at it.
- **Neglect:** If the duelist fought in none of the forest's battles, he refuses the duel: *"You brought me a stranger."* The player may come back after one battle.
- **The duel:** one on one at the forest's end.
- **Endings:** **Win:** the move **Samurai Slash**: Bug, physical, 60 power; the first time it is used in a battle it strikes first and always flinches; after that it is a plain 60-power move. **Win with a duelist that evolved inside the forest:** Samurai Slash *and* a Bug-type egg (Scyther, Pinsir or Heracross). **Lose:** he bows and waits at the exit of the next forest-type stage for a rematch.
- **Afterstory:** A Samurai who lost appears later in the run with a Bug-type that plainly copied the player's.

### 4. Mt. Moon
*(Pseudo Stage with three episodes)*

- **Clefairy Moon Watching.** Three dialogue choices: stay still, stay quiet, look up. Three right: a Clefairy with *Moonblast*. Two right: a Moon Stone. Fewer: they scatter. The moon's phase comes from the stage number; at full moon two right answers are enough. Sometimes a Clefairy takes one of the player's Pokémon for one of its own and tries to lead it away by the hand.
- **Fossil Recovery.** Two researchers each insist the other's fossil is a fake. The player battles one; the loser's fossil is the prize. A third fossil sits on the table that both call worthless: asking for it instead skips the battle, and it is drawn from the rarer half of the fossil table.
- **Gem-Stone Mine.** Three swings per visit; each swing draws a Rock-type encounter.

| Move type used | Deposit | Find |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fighting | Ore face | A random evolution stone |
| Steel | Reinforced seam | Hard Stone, Metal Coat or Everstone |
| Ground | Buried pocket | Fire, Water, Thunder or Leaf Stone |

- **Secret:** With a Clefairy or Clefable in the party, 25% to see the full moon dance: a second Moon Stone and a Cleffa egg.

### 5. Damian's Unofficial Gym
*(Optional event node; Region-Wide Story across runs)*

- **The Gym:** A barn. Cardboard pillars. A hand-painted sign with "Official" spelled wrong and corrected. The theme is whatever Damian liked that week.
- **The fights:** Two friends who barely understand battling (two Pokémon each, two levels under the player; one asks which button is "the attack one"). Then Damian: three Pokémon and a real ace at the player's level +1, with a moveset that has clearly been thought about.
- **Reward:** The **Bottle-Cap Badge** (a keepsake: +5% prize money from trainers). If the player loses, Damian gives a motivational speech he obviously needs more than they do, and the node can be retried.
- **Recurrence:** He appears at most once per region and improves each time: the barn, then a rented hall with one real Gym trainer, then an inspected Gym. On the third meeting he has a legitimate team and a real badge, and he does not go easy.
- **Afterstory:** His two friends are in the Final Reunion, wearing staff shirts.

### 7. Cold Night's Rain

- **The stage:** Permanent night and rain. The Fire-type rides along and does not battle. It has a **Flame** from 0 to 5.

| Action | Flame |
| --- | --- |
| Build a shelter at a rest | +1 |
| Offer food (it refuses the first time; offer again) | +1 |
| One of the player's own Fire-types sits with it | +2 |
| Win a battle without letting anyone faint | +1 |
| Leave it out in the open at a rest | −1 |

- **The owner:** At the end, its former trainer comes back for it now that it "looks stronger". At Flame 3 it steps between them. At Flame 5 it fights the trainer's last Pokémon itself.
- **Reward:** It warms to the player and asks to join, with **Kept Flame**: its Fire moves are not weakened by rain.
- **If the player walked on:** somebody else saves it. It is met later, healthy, beside a nurse. It looks at the player for a moment.

### 8. The Squirtle Squad
*(Miniature Episode, Community Episode)*

- **Fights:** three Lesser members, then the Leader Wartortle. If the party average is level 36 or more, a Leader Blastoise; at 50 or more, a Mega Squad Blastoise.
- **The five:** the Leader (takes everything seriously), one who keeps losing its shades, one who is sure it is the strongest, one who is terrified of fire, and one who wants to be a performer. Between fights the player gets one short exchange with each; the right response earns **Respect**.
- **Recruitment:** A Squad member is offered with its shades. At Respect 3 or more it is your 50/50 between Lesser and Leader; below that it is a Lesser member.
- **The recruit:** Knows *Swagger* and *Hex*; ability **Contrary**. Two small rules make that kit work: for this Squirtle, *Hex* treats confusion as a status condition; and it knows **Shades Down (new)**: Dark, special, 90 power, lowers the user's Sp. Atk by one stage, which Contrary turns into a boost.
- **Afterstory:** A Lesser recruit that reaches the Leader's level may go back and challenge it. If it wins, it returns to the player in the Leader's shades.

### 9. Bill's Lighthouse
*(Pseudo Stage; Mystery Episode)*

- **The mystery:** The lighthouse is sending a signal out to sea, and something is answering. Wild Pokémon gather on the shore at night, facing the water.
- **Five nodes:** the shore (a huge shape passes under the surface); the keeper's cottage (old photographs, some of Pokémon from regions the player has already finished); the stairs (research logs in three hands, one of them a child's); the lamp room (a three-pattern signal puzzle: match each light pattern to a recorded cry); the gallery, at dawn.
- **Ending:** The player helps Bill work out what has been answering. It surfaces once. The species is drawn from a short table: a very large Dragonite, a Lapras pod, a Wailord, or (1 in 100) a silhouette that Bill refuses to name.
- **Reward:** Access to Bill's PC, as in your draft; and **Bill's Recorder**, a key item that reveals one unknown encounter per stage.
- **Afterstory:** Bill writes once in a later region. He has heard it again.

### 10. The S.S. Anne
*(Pseudo Stage; Hub Destination)*

- **Shape:** Three decks, four nodes each. Every node is a trainer battle and offers **two trades**: one ordinary, and one for a regional Pokémon or a Pokémon carrying the region gene.
- **Optional rooms (pick up to four per voyage):** trainer cabins; the Pokémon dining room; a deck competition; the game room; a beauty contest; a seasick Pokémon that needs fresh air and someone to sit with; a collector who wants to buy one of the player's Pokémon and raises the offer twice; a trainer who challenges the player on every deck and gets a little better; a secret trade room behind the kitchen; and, below the waterline, a Rocket operation loading crates.
- **The end:** A rival battle on the top deck, with dialogue drawn from the run so far. Then the captain's gift.
- **The Network Machine:** A TM that teaches its Pokémon **any one move it can legally learn** by level, egg, tutor or TM, then is used up. It cannot teach signature moves, boss-only moves or event-only moves. One per run.
- **Afterstory:** Passengers from the voyage reappear across the region. The collector is still asking.

### 11. Kaiju Tentacruel
*(World Event; raid)*

- **Cold open:** A pier folds in half.
- **The raid (three phases, not Dynamax):**
  1. **The harbour.** Tentacool swarm in the shallows. Stranded trainers can be rescued; each one rescued gives one assist turn later.
  2. **The sea wall.** Pylons and cranes can be knocked onto it for a one-turn stun. Each one used makes it angrier (+1 Attack).
  3. **The reef.** It stops attacking the town and starts guarding one spot.
- **What is there:** an injured Tentacool, caught in a net.
- **Endings:** **Defeat it:** a Tentacool with **Huge Power** and *Liquidation*, as in your draft. **Free the injured one** (two turns protecting it while a party Pokémon cuts the net): the same reward, but it is *that* Tentacool, and the Kaiju turns up once more as an ally in a sea battle. **Find the cause** (the net is marked with a factory stamp): this links to the Polluted Factory and changes its ending.
- **Afterstory:** The town rebuilds the pier with a gap in it, for something to swim through.

### 12. Maiden's Peak
*(Pseudo Stage; Hub Destination; Mystery Episode)*

- **The market** is walkable, like Hub Town: a shop that sells **specific** move TMs, a trading district, an egg district, costume stalls, mystery boxes, Ghost-type vendors who only open at dusk, and a fortune teller who says one true thing about the next stage.
- **The lady** is seen five times across the market, always at the far end of a street. Each time the player reaches the spot she is gone, and something small is left: a petal, a ribbon, a wet footprint.

### 13. Flight North
*(Interrupting Episode; Pseudo Stage; Companion Story; Finale)*

- **The check:** Mandatory. 50% nothing. 50% Jessie, James and Meowth take every Pokémon the player has.
- **The stage (levels 5 to 20):** The player wakes with nothing. A Spearow bullies them. A Caterpie or Weedle knocks it out of the air and then looks as surprised as the player. This Bug-type is the only Pokémon for the stage.
- **It is a character before anything is lost.** It has a name (the player may change it), its own idle animation, a cry the player will know later, and a small victory hop. It evolves along the way; if it is not fully evolved by the final battle it evolves mid-battle, for its trainer.
- **The end:** Against the trio's Venomoth. If the player is losing, a shiny one of the same species arrives and ends it. The player's team is returned at full health. The Bug-type says goodbye.
- **The return:** Later, if the player loses the run: the game-over screen cracks, cracks again, and shatters. It is there, as a Mega Beedrill or a Gigantamax-sized Butterfree. It revives the whole team and fights alongside for that battle. This happens **once per run** and has its own music.
- **After that battle:** the player may keep it. It always has its Mega Stone, and knows **North's Wind**: revives one fainted team member at 50% HP, then raises the user's Sp. Atk and Speed by one stage. **One charge per battle; the charge refills only at a rest.**
- **Endings:** Keep it. Release it (its silhouette crosses the sky in later regions). Secret: if its Bond is at maximum it refuses to leave.
- **Order of rescue:** If this return is available it is used first; the 5% Second Chance sequence in `RIVAL_SYSTEM_OVERHAUL.md` cannot also trigger in the same run.

### 14. Sabrina's Labyrinth
*(Pseudo Stage; Mystery Episode; Companion Story. An event encounter, not a Gym battle.)*

- **Start:** The party is scattered across the stage. The first fight cannot be won. A tombstone. A swarm of Ghost-types crosses the screen.
- **The companion:** A Shuppet, Duskull, Drifloon or Gastly offers to help the player get their body back. It is the only usable Pokémon. The player collects their own Pokémon along the way but cannot use them yet.
- **Losing as a ghost** resets the stage's progress, as in your draft. *(A softer setting, "resume from the last Pokémon found", is worth having for accessibility.)*
- **The rooms:** the player's own body standing in a room they have not entered; party Pokémon glimpsed as ghosts; doors that open onto places from earlier regions; a villager repeating one sentence; children's drawings of the player's team; a small grave for a Pokémon the player never lost. The companion starts by copying the player's movements and ends by leading.
- **Sabrina and Alakazam:** A battle the player cannot command normally. Each turn one of the four move slots is false (it shows one move and does another). Every second turn the companion can "see true" and mark the false slot.
- **If the player is about to lose:** the companion calls every Ghost-type in the building. Alakazam goes down under them, and the player has their body back. The companion says goodbye and is gone.
- **Next region, at the parallel stage:** black screen; a jump scare; it asks to join. It holds its Mega Stone if it has one, and knows **Haunting**: Ghost, special, 80 power, and the target becomes **Haunted**.
- **Haunted (a fixed four-turn cycle, so it stays fair):**

| Turn | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | One random stat falls by one stage |
| 2 | 50% chance to become confused |
| 3 | One random stat falls by one stage |
| 4 | 50% chance to become confused; Haunted ends |

  It cannot be re-applied to the same target for two turns after it ends. Bosses are Haunted for two turns only.

### 16. The Fighting Tournament
*(Tournament Episode; needs the Route 25 Mankey)*

- **Format:** 32 entrants, five rounds, Fighting-types only, every opponent scaled exactly to the player's Mankey or Primeape. Only that Pokémon may enter.
- **A sports anime in five rounds:** commentary; a crowd; a training montage between rounds where the player picks **Power**, **Guard** or **Footwork** (a small bonus for the rest of the bracket); and recurring competitors: a Hitmonchan that has never made a sound, a Machop whose twin coaches from the rail, a Hawlucha that plays to the crowd and is better than it pretends, a Throh that bows to everyone including the referee.
- **The final** is always a rival Mankey or Primeape, with its own reasons for being there. If either fighter has not evolved, it evolves in the heat of the match.
- **Reward:** the title, *Dynamic Punch*, the **belt form** (a cosmetic form, the same species; it never evolves into Annihilape), and the ability **Champion's Entrance**: on entry, one random stat rises by two stages (never the same stat twice running, and never more than +4 in total per battle), and its first attack after entering has +1 priority.
- **Afterstory:** The rival turns up in a later region wearing a home-made belt.

### 17. The Polluted Factory
*(Pseudo Stage; raid)*

- **The cause:** The factory makes cut-price Poké Balls and dumps the solvent. The same stamp is on the net in Kaiju Tentacruel.
- **Inside, the player may:** shut down the line; free Pokémon trapped in the sludge pits; take the research; fight the scientists; help an injured Muk that is trying to hold a leak shut with its own body; and discover that the runoff has produced a new form.
- **Magmuk (uncatchable; three phases):** *Sludge* (it splits when hit by spread moves), *Furnace* (it is burning and so is the floor), *Core* (it is small, fast and frightened). If the Muk was helped, it absorbs one phase's first attack.
- **Reward:** Black Sludge, and a choice of Alolan Grimer, Galarian Grimer or Galarian Trubbish.
- **Consequences:** **Shut down:** for the rest of the region, Poison-types are a step rarer on water routes and Water-types a step more common. **Research taken:** it can be sold, or handed to a professor to unlock one special Ball. **Left running:** the next coastal event is worse.

### 19. The Deep Savanna
*(Pseudo Stage)*

- **Identity:** Ground-types and sand. Herd migrations cross the map and block nodes for a turn. Territorial Ground-types guard the waterholes. At night the predators come out and the encounter table changes. It has not rained; water is a resource the player carries, and wild Pokémon will trade for it.
- **Non-hostile nodes:** at least two per visit where nothing attacks and the player can simply watch.

### 20. The Lush Jungle
*(Pseudo Stage)*

## B. Region-wide and companion stories

### 21. The Pokémon That Wouldn't Evolve
*(Region-Wide Story, Companion Story)*

- **The disagreement:** A trainer is holding an Evolution Stone out to a Pikachu, Piplup, Torchic or other unevolved Pokémon, and it has its back turned. The trainer is not a villain. They are worried it will get hurt.
- **The player may** agree with the trainer, defend the Pokémon, or say nothing. The game does not say who is right. If defended, the Pokémon challenges the player's strongest Pokémon to prove the point, and loses, narrowly.
- **Mid-region:** It returns and shows a powerful new move, still unevolved.
- **Region's end:** The trainer tries to force the evolution. The player may step in.
- **Endings:**
  - It stays as it is and joins with **Unwavering**: all its stats are raised by 25% while it is unevolved, and it can never be evolved. *(Does not stack with Eviolite; the larger bonus applies.)*
  - It stays with its trainer, who puts the Stone away.
  - It evolves by its own choice, later, off-screen, and sends word.
  - It evolves in a crisis, to protect its trainer, and is not sorry.
  - It leaves its trainer and joins the player.
- **Exclusive move, Proof:** 80 power, the user's own primary type; +20% against a fully evolved target.
- **Afterstory:** The trainer is met again. The Stone is on a string round their neck, unused.

### 22. The Lonely Cubone
*(Pseudo Stage; Miniature Episode; Companion Story)*

- **Opening:** A Cubone alone in the woods. Approach it (it runs), leave an item at its feet (it looks), or walk away.
- **What it wants:** It draws in the dirt: a larger shape, a mound, a question. It is looking for its mother's grave. It follows the player as an ally for the stage, and draws at each rest. The drawings get more detailed as it settles.
- **If it faints,** the stage ends and it runs off crying, as in your draft. It can be found once more, in the next region, more wary.
- **The end:** Mandibuzz and Vullaby come for it, in three waves. The player must keep it standing.
- **The grave:** There is a place where she *may* be. There is no proof. The player can say it is, say they do not know, or sit down beside it and say nothing. The last one is the best ending.
- **Reward:** It may join. If the player gives it a **Thick Club**, it first shows them the one it has been carrying all along, then takes the new one in the other hand. It gains **Inherited Strength**: Attack +1 whenever an ally faints, to +2 per battle.
- **Afterstory:** A recurring graveyard node in later regions. If Cubone is in the party, it stops there.

### 24. The Forgotten Partner
*(Interrupting Episode; Reunion Episode)*

- **Who:** A retired trainer recognises the Pokémon the player has owned longest that was caught wild.
- **Two stories:** The trainer tells how they parted. The Pokémon's reaction tells its own version: at high Bond it greets them warmly and comes back to the player's side; at low Bond it goes to them and stays there a while; and sometimes it flinches.
- **The player may** let them spend time together, refuse, ask questions (three, each revealing a difference between the two stories), let the Pokémon decide, or come back over several visits and help them make peace.
- **Reward:** The trainer teaches a move the Pokémon used to know, tied to its history. At high Bond it learns **Two Trainers**: 75 power, its own type; 100 power at maximum Bond.
- **The risk, only if the player chooses "let it decide" at the lowest Bond:** it may go home. The player receives a keepsake and a young Pokémon the trainer has been raising.

### 25. The Unlikely Friendship
*(Miniature Episode, Companion Story)*

- **What the player sees:** A Zangoose and a Seviper asleep in a heap. They share food, squabble over who walks in front, and put themselves between each other and anything that looks dangerous.
- **The fight:** A pack attacks; the player fights alongside both.
- **Endings:** Recruit one (the other is glimpsed in the background of later stages, and can be recruited at the third sighting); recruit both; leave them together.
- **Paired ability, Truce:** active while both are in the party in adjacent slots. In a Double Battle each takes 25% less damage from moves that hit both. In a Single Battle, when one faints the other gains Attack +1 and Speed +1. Once per battle.

### 26. The Island of Giant Pokémon
*(Pseudo Stage)*

- **An ecosystem, not a boss rush.** Giants are not Dynamax or Gigantamax: double HP, three-quarter Speed, their own animations. A giant Pidgeot lets small Flying-types nest on its back. A giant Snorlax has dammed a river by lying down. Some are gentle, some territorial, some just curious and far too large to be curious safely.
- **The guardian:** a giant Nidoking. It guards the island; it is not its villain. The player may fight it, or earn passage by solving three of the island's problems (the river, a nest, a quarrel).
- **Reward:** **Giant's Mark** (held item): HP +25%, Speed −15%.
- **Afterstory:** A single enormous footprint is found on the mainland in a later region. Nobody comments on it.

### 27. The Pokémon Circus
*(Hub Destination; recurring)*

| Attraction | What it is | Reward |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mr. Mime's illusions | Spot which of three is real | A held item |
| Machamp's strength contest | Enter one Pokémon; Attack decides | A stat item |
| Rapidash's track | A short race; Speed and timing | Prize money |
| The jugglers | A rhythm game | A Berry bundle |
| The arena | Three battles, no switching | A TM |
| The mystery tent | One unknown Pokémon, offered once | The Pokémon |

- **The final act:** a battle with the circus owner, whose team is the performers.
- **The shy one:** a performer that is talented and terrified of crowds. The player can help it: rehearse in the empty tent, stand in the wings, or go on with it.
- **Recurring:** the circus returns in later regions with different acts. If the shy one was helped, it is top of the bill, and it finds the player in the audience.

### 28. The Team Rocket Hideout
*(Pseudo Stage; evil team)*

- **Four floors:** offices, labs, holding, vault. Security cameras fill an **Alert** meter; at full, every remaining Grunt has one more Pokémon. Optional treasure rooms. One locked laboratory, opened by a key carried by a scientist who is having second thoughts.
- **End:** a Rocket Executive.
- **Rewards (choose one):** a Shadow Pokémon (optional, and never forced; the Shadow rules in the pack apply), a stolen evolution item, a rare egg, or a pass to the black-market shop.
- **The rescued cast:** Pokémon freed here are remembered. Some recognise the player in later stages; a few ask to join; the rest found a rescue centre that becomes a rest node.
- **The scientist:** spared and listened to, they become an informant in the next region.

### 29. The Psyduck Migration
*(Miniature Episode; Snake minigame)*

- **The town:** Psyduck in the road, in the fountain, on a roof. Stray Confusion is rearranging the street furniture.
- **The game:** Snake. The player is the head; each Psyduck collected joins the line. Three rounds, each faster, each with more things for the line to knock over. A Golduck in the party walks at the back and stops the tail from wandering.
- **Reward:** The most confused Psyduck, with **Headache**: when it is hit by a critical or super-effective hit, Sp. Atk +1, to +2 per battle. And **a bottle of Ibuprofen**: a one-of-a-kind keepsake, not medicine. Once per stage, outside battle, it clears one Pokémon's confusion-prone state: that Pokémon cannot be confused in its next battle.

### 30. The Island of Pink Pokémon
*(Pseudo Stage)*

- **A real population.** They are pink because of a mineral in the island's fruit, and they behave like islanders: they move in mixed groups, they are unafraid, and a few are fiercely territorial about one particular grove.
- **The Pink Slowking** is studying them and thinks slowly and very well. Its challenge is a quiz of three observations about the island (the answers are things the player could have noticed) and then a battle.
- **Reward:** a rare pink encounter, or a cosmetic pink variant of one Pokémon the player already owns. Pink is cosmetic.
- **Afterstory:** A pink Pokémon fed mainland food for a full region slowly loses its colour, unless the player kept some fruit.

### 31. The Forgotten Forest
*(Pseudo Stage; Legendary-adjacent)*

- **Identity:** untouched for centuries. Trees with rooms in them. Ruins with carvings of migrations that no longer happen. Rare Grass-types, Bug colonies, clearings the map does not show.
- **End:** an ancient Trevenant guarding a sacred tree. It can be fought, or satisfied by showing it that the player has harmed nothing here.
- **Reward:** an ancient Grass-type with an unusual ability.
- **The two secrets roll separately:** 5% for a Celebi sighting, 5% for a Tapu Koko sighting. Either is **shiny only if the player knocked out no wild Pokémon in the forest** (catching and fleeing are fine). Nothing in the game says so.

### 32. The Crystal Caverns
*(Pseudo Stage)*

| Crystal | Effect while carried (this stage only) |
| --- | --- |
| Red | One Pokémon's moves of its own type gain 10% |
| Blue | Temporarily changes one Pokémon's secondary type |
| Green | Lets one Pokémon evolve without its usual condition |
| White | Shows hidden paths |
| Black | Wild Pokémon behave strangely near it: some flee, some follow |

- **Also:** Rock-, Ice- and Steel-types; ore deposits; light-beam puzzles.
- **The Crystal Golem:** a Gigalith with raised stats, in three phases. Each phase it takes the type of the crystal the player is carrying the most of, so the player's own pockets decide the fight.

### 33. The Sunken City
*(Pseudo Stage; Mystery Episode)*

- **The city tells it:** three districts, entered in any order. The market has stalls laid out as if for tomorrow. The houses have high-water marks carved and dated, each a little higher. The temple has a mural with the sea drawn inside the walls, and the people walking out.
- **What happened:** nobody drowned. The water rose for a hundred years and they left in good order. They left one thing behind on purpose.
- **Rules:** Water-types open certain paths. Old machinery still runs and can be switched on to drain a room or flood one.
- **End:** an ancient Relicanth. It is not guarding treasure. It is guarding the last record of who lived here. It can be fought or shown respect (the player returns three objects to where they belong).
- **Reward:** a mysterious item that reacts in the next stage.

### 34. Caverns-o-Mighty
*(Pseudo Stage; follow-up to the Sunken City)*

- **The item reacts** and a cave mouth opens in the ground.
- **The Unown are the cave.** They are the only wild Pokémon, and they do more than fight: they spell words along the walls, they change which door leads where, and once, in a long corridor, they arrange themselves into the player's name.
- **The puzzle:** three chambers, each asking for a word. The answers are in the Sunken City's carvings.
- **End:** one of the Regi Pokémon (including your Deltas), chosen by the last word the player spelled. It can be caught or defeated. A player who guessed instead of reading gets the battle with no clue which one it will be.

### 35. Thunderstorm Ridge
*(Pseudo Stage)*

- **Lightning:** 20% per node to strike the party's lead and paralyse it. A Ground-type in front, or a lightning-rod item, prevents it.
- **Charged crystals:** carrying one draws Electric-types to the player, friendly and otherwise.
- **Weather puzzles:** routes that are only open between strikes; a metal bridge to cross at the right moment.
- **End:** a rare Electric-type that has taken in far more than it can hold. It is in pain and it is dangerous. The player may defeat it, or **earth it**: keep a Ground-type on the field for three turns while it discharges.
- **Reward:** a special Ability Capsule (it can grant a hidden ability). If it was earthed, it follows the player down the mountain.

### 36. The Endless Desert
*(Pseudo Stage; Region-Wide Story)*

- **Rules:** Ground-, Rock- and Fire-types. Sandstorms hide the next node's type. A water supply that falls each node and is refilled at oases; at zero the party enters each battle at 90% HP. Mirages lead to treasure or a battle; each has a tell for players who look.
- **The traveller:** met at three oases, in a different state each time (lost, robbed, laughing at something). On the third meeting they explain: a Flygon carried them out of this desert when they were a child, and they have come back to thank it.
- **End:** an ancient Flygon guarding an oasis. If the traveller is with the player, there is no battle.
- **Reward:** a Trapinch from the oasis, and buried-ruin items.

### 37. The Haunted Mansion
*(Pseudo Stage; Mystery Episode)*

- **Four floors,** a different Ghost-type on each. Furniture has moved when the player looks back. The music stops for a few seconds for no reason. One door opens onto the room the player just left. A portrait's expression changes between visits. Something is behind the player in one mirror, once. A drawer holds a dozen old Poké Balls, all empty, all polished.
- **The owner** greets the player on the top floor. It is a Mimikyu in the owner's coat. It has been keeping the house the way it was, for someone who is not coming back.
- **It is lonely, not malicious.** The player may battle it, or stay for the tea it has laid out.
- **Reward:** the Mimikyu, with **Borrowed Face**: its Disguise is restored once per battle when it knocks out a foe; or the move **Welcome Home**: Ghost, 70 power, and the target cannot switch out next turn.

### 38. The Overgrown Laboratory
*(Pseudo Stage; Companion Story)*

- **Setting:** an abandoned facility taken back by plants. Notes on the walls, getting less confident page by page. Empty containment cells. Escaped Pokémon with abilities they should not have.
- **The subject:** the final room holds the experimental Pokémon. It was made to be strong and nobody told it what for. It asks, in its own way, what it is supposed to do now.
- **Endings:** help it leave; help the returning researchers (one of whom wants to finish the work, and one of whom wants to apologise); or shut the facility down.
- **Its form reflects the player:** a regional variant if it was freed, a custom form if the work was finished, an unusual evolution if the player simply stayed with it until it decided.

## C. Hubs and challenges

### 39. The Travelling Pokémon Market
*(Hub Destination; recurring)*

- **Stalls:** Evolution Stones, held items, TMs, trades, eggs, mystery items, rare encounters. Stock changes on every appearance.
- **Merchants remember.** A vendor the player bought from offers 10% off next time; one the player haggled down too hard is "out of stock". Two vendors are rivals and undercut each other if the player mentions the other's price.
- **Conditional stalls:** one that appears only at night, one only if the player carries a fossil, one only after an evil-team win.

### 40. The Pokémon Day-Care Village
*(Hub Destination; Community Episode)*

- **Services:** leave two Pokémon; buy eggs; learn egg moves; breeding challenges.
- **Life:** families of Pokémon that have lived here for generations; a hatching celebration the whole village attends; hatchlings that inherit a habit from a parent (shown as a small idle animation).
- **Adoption:** each visit has one egg with a story (found in a storm, left on a doorstep, the last of a clutch). The breeder asks the player one question before handing it over.
- **Special breeder:** offers a rare Pokémon if the player arrives with a specific pair.

### 41. The Pokémon Museum
*(Hub Destination; Mystery Episode)*

- **Exhibits:** fossils, artifacts, a hall of Legendary Pokémon, regional history.
- **Interactive:** fossil identification (a correct answer wins the fossil's cast, which a lab can revive); fossil restoration (a jigsaw); hidden items behind exhibits.
- **The mystery:** one label in the museum is wrong, on purpose. Finding it and telling the curator unlocks a locked gallery and a secret encounter.
- **Blessings:** the Legendary hall adds 1 Influence to the player's Legendary Blessing if its Legendary is on display.

### 42. The Battle Frontier
*(Hub Destination; Tournament Episode)*

| Building | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Tower | Seven battles in a row |
| Factory | Rental Pokémon only |
| Arena | Three turns, then judges decide |
| Pike | Choose doors; some heal, some hurt |
| Dome | Bracket; the player sees opposing teams first |
| Palace | Pokémon choose their own moves by nature |
| Pyramid | Dark floors; items found inside only |

- **Structure:** the player clears one building or several, then meets its Frontier Brain. Brains recur across runs and remember the last team the player brought. A **rank** rises with each building cleared and decides the Brain's team strength.

### 43. The Pokémon Auction
*(Community Episode; Mystery Episode)*

- **Live bidding** for held items, Stones, fossils, eggs, regional variants and unusual Pokémon.
- **Bidders have personalities:** one never goes past a round number; one bids on everything and drops out late; one wants a single lot and will overpay; one is bidding with someone else's money.
- **The stolen lot:** one item is stolen property. The clues are in the catalogue. The player may buy it and return it, expose it mid-auction, or say nothing.
- **Secret lot:** offered only to a player who exposed a stolen lot at an earlier auction.

### 44. The 1v1 Championship
*(Tournament Episode)*

- **Rules:** one Pokémon, eight opponents, all scaled to it, no items between battles.
- **Reward:** a title and an ability upgrade.
- **Recurring:** two of the eight are the same trainers every time, and they remember which Pokémon the player entered before. The reigning champion comments if it is the same one.

### 45. The Champion's Training Session
*(Companion Story)*

- **Lesson:** a weakened version of the Champion's team, with the Champion explaining each decision as they make it.
- **Montage:** one of the player's Pokémon is singled out for individual work and gains a small permanent bonus.
- **Final exam (optional):** the full team. A win: the Champion teaches the player's ace an exclusive move.

### 46. The All-Star Tournament
*(Tournament Episode; Reunion Episode)*

- **Entrants** are trainers the player has already met this run, with upgraded teams and dialogue about the last meeting. Trainers the player lost to are seeded higher.
- **Final:** the player's strongest rival.
- **Reward:** a trophy and a rare encounter.

### 47. The Pokémon League Exhibition
*(Onlooker)*

- **Matchups:** Red and Blue; Lance and Steven; Cynthia and Iris; Leon and Diantha; Wallace and Alder.
- **Watching matters:** the winner may reward the player depending on what the player said before the match. Sometimes the player is invited to face the winner.
- **Ripples:** the result changes what trainers in the next stage are talking about, and one trainer copies a move they saw.

### 48. The Pokémon Draft
*(Tournament Episode)*

- **Loop:** pick one Pokémon from a rotating selection; fight; pick another; repeat to six. The final bracket uses only the drafted team.
- **Reward:** recruit one drafted Pokémon.
- **Memory:** a drafted Pokémon remembers its original trainer. If the player later releases or trades it, it goes back.

## D. The rest

### 49. The Sleeping Snorlax
*(Interrupting Episode)*

- **Options:** Poké Flute, food, battle, or leave.
- **Music:** if the flute is not enough, the player must make noise into the microphone until it wakes. *(Always offer a button-press alternative for players without a microphone or who cannot shout.)*
- **How it wakes decides who it is:** by music, friendly; by food, friendly and now following the smell of the player's bag; by battle, angry.
- **Reward:** a friendly Snorlax may join with **Heavy Sleeper**: it restores 1/8 of its HP at the end of any turn in which it did not use a damaging move.
- **Afterstory:** a Snorlax that did not join is found asleep across the road in a later region. It opens one eye, recognises the player, and rolls over to let them past.

### 50. The Evil Team's Betrayal
*(Evil team; Interrupting Episode; Region-Wide Story)*

- **The offer:** a member of an evil team says the organisation is about to sell them out, and asks for help stealing the documents that prove it.
- **Trust (0 to 5):** rises when the player keeps their word and falls when they check up on the defector. At 3, the defector shares routes (one free scouting of an evil-team node per stage). At 5, they stand beside the player in a climax.
- **Expose them instead:** a reward from the team's enemies now, and the defector returns as a boss later, with a team built against the player's.
- **Their own story:** between meetings they are doing something the player only hears about. It matters at the end.

### 51. The Pokémon Smuggling Ring
*(Evil team)*

- **The scene:** cages in a warehouse; regional forms being moved by night; guards with strong Pokémon.
- **Reward:** a rescued Pokémon with a special ability.
- **Rehabilitation:** a rescued Pokémon may be afraid of Poké Balls. It walks beside the player and will not go inside. After three rests of patience it climbs in by itself, and its Bond starts high.

### 52. The Evil Team's Pokémon Auction
*(Evil team)*

- **Undercover:** the player picks a disguise (a rich buyer, a rival team's agent, a caterer). Each opens different rooms.
- **The bidding:** several lots must be won. Some bids are traps set to make a newcomer show how much money they have. Some bidders are not who they say either.
- **The rare Pokémon:** it can be bought fairly, or rescued without spending anything by a route the caterer disguise finds.

### 53. The Region Under Siege
*(Interrupting Episode; World Event)*

- **Shape:** a major city, several districts, a clock. NPC trainers the player has met join the defence. The player cannot be everywhere.
- **Choices are permanent for the run:** a district that is not defended is damaged (its shop and services are gone); a civilian who is not reached is missing from later scenes; a Pokémon that is rescued is waiting at the next rest.
- **End:** the team's commander. The result decides the region's next stage: a city in celebration, a city in repair, or a city under occupation that the player must pass through quietly.

### 54. The Magikarp Convention
*(Community Episode)*

- **What there is:** synchronised jumping; a jumping contest the player can enter; merchandise; a trainer with six Magikarp who is entirely serious.
- **Regulars:** five Magikarp with names and habits appear every time, and their scores are remembered between runs.
- **The secret:** mid-competition, one of them evolves. The crowd goes silent, then wild. If it is the one from *The Magikarp That Could*, it looks for the player.

### 55. The Angry Bidoof
*(Miniature Episode)*

- **The insult:** a trainer laughed at it.
- **The battle:** very high stats, several strong moves. Its own trainer stands behind it explaining, with complete sincerity, that it is a perfectly ordinary Bidoof and that Bidoof are simply misunderstood.
- **Reward:** if defeated, it respects the player and joins with **Unbothered**: its stats cannot be lowered by opponents.
- **Afterstory:** the trainer who laughed is met later and crosses the road.

### 56. The Slowpoke Detective Agency
*(Mystery Episode; recurring comedy)*

- **The case:** a missing item. Clues are everywhere; half are useless; the Slowpoke weigh the useless half more heavily.
- **The pattern:** they misread everything and arrive, by accident, at the truth and a rare Pokémon.
- **Recurring:** each new case is more serious than the last (a missing hat, a missing shipment, a missing person). The Slowpoke do not notice the difference. By the third case, real detectives are asking how they do it.

### 57. The Pokémon Impersonator
*(Mystery Episode)*

- **The disguise:** a Zoroark as a famous Champion, giving advice that is nearly right.
- **Tells:** it uses the wrong Pokémon's name once; it casts no shadow in one frame; it knows something only a wild Pokémon would.
- **Endings:** expose it (a battle), or play along and befriend it (it joins).
- **Reward:** **Many Faces**: its Illusion re-forms once per battle when it switches back in.
- **Later:** it may imitate a rival, a Champion, or the player. The party can tell. The player sometimes cannot.

### 58. Old Friend Returns
*(Reunion Episode)*

- **Rule:** every released Pokémon keeps an identity: its nickname, where it was let go, and why.
- **The meeting:** it recognises the player. Its level and evolution show the time that has passed. It may have a new trainer, who is told nothing or everything, at the player's choice.
- **What it does:** challenges the player, or helps in the next hard fight. A Pokémon released at the end of an emotional storyline has its own scene.

### 59. The Former Champion
*(Region-Wide Story across regions)*

- **Who:** a retired Champion who now travels. Their Pokémon are old and still strong. They talk more than they battle.
- **Each meeting:** a story, a piece of advice that pays off later, and an optional battle against a team that is one Pokémon short.
- **The request:** in the last region they ask the player to help find one final Pokémon. It is not a rare one. It is the species of the first Pokémon they ever lost.

### 60. The Pokémon That Followed You Home
*(Region-Wide Story across regions)*

- **It never interacts.** It is a shape at the edge of a background, then a tail leaving a frame, then a berry left by the fire, then footprints beside the player's own.
- **The reveal:** after enough regions it simply walks out and sits down.
- **Reward:** it joins with **Long Journey**: all its stats rise by 3% for each region the player has completed, to a cap of 12%.

### 61. The Final Reunion
*(Finale Episode; Reunion Episode)*

- **Who comes:** rivals, Gym Leaders, released Pokémon, rescued Pokémon, friendly NPCs, travelling trainers; and from this document and the new events, everyone with an afterstory.
- **What the player does:** walk among them and talk. No battle is required.
- **Who helps:** up to three may join the final encounter, one turn each, chosen by the strength of the player's history with them.
- **Pairs with** *The Long Table* in `GREENNEEDLE_NEW_EVENTS.md`.

### 62. The Pokémon Prison
*(Pseudo Stage)*

- **Start:** the team is taken. The player has nothing.
- **Stealth:** guards walk fixed routes that can be learned; disguises open some doors; keys and equipment can be stolen; being seen costs time, not the run.
- **The roster:** the player's Pokémon are in holding cells in different wings, with strangers' Pokémon beside them. The player chooses who to free first, and whether to open the other cells too.
- **End:** the warden. Every rescued Pokémon is returned to the party; any the player did not reach is returned after the battle, and it noticed.
- **Afterstory:** strangers' Pokémon the player freed are met again, with their trainers, who know the player's name.
