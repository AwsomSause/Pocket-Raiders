# WHOLE RUN EVENTS

**Builds on:** `Whole_Run_Events.md`.
**What this adds:** the framework all five share; *Guidance of Giratina* written across an entire run, with its counters, ten beats, the Distortion Division, the Avatar of Distant Death, the reveal, six endings and his breakdown; and four more Whole Run Events to fill your limit of five.
*Numbers are starting values. Nothing here has been playtested.*

> **Content note.** *Guidance of Giratina* is a horror story about a cult. It contains death, coercion, body horror and the indoctrination of children. Section 2.11 gives three intensity settings. Two choices I made, so you can overrule them: **harm to children is never shown**, only its aftermath; and the leader's breakdown is **an original speech** written in the spirit you asked for. I have not copied lines from Dragon Ball Super, and I would not copy them into a game you may share. The outline of that kind of speech (a self-made god refusing to accept that he lost) is free for anyone to use, and that is what is here.

---

## 1. The framework

### 1.1 What a Whole Run Event is

A story that begins near the start of a run, returns again and again, and ends in a climax **immediately before the final Champion**. It is driven by decisions. One small choice, inside the event or outside it, can turn the whole thing.

### 1.2 Rules

1. **Five exist. One per run at most.**
2. **Chance:** 35% of runs draw one. They are dealt from a shuffled bag, so a player sees all five before any repeats.
3. **It can be declined at the door.** Every Whole Run Event opens with a forced meeting and two large boxes. One box engages. The other does not make the story go away: it makes it happen *to* the player instead of *with* them.
4. **Ten beats.** One forced opening, eight returns, one climax, placed on a fixed skeleton:

| Beat | Where | Kind |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Stage 2 | Forced meeting, two boxes |
| 2 | Region 1, middle | A place changed by the story |
| 3 | Region 1, end | The first dilemma |
| 4 | Region 2, early | An ally, or an explanation |
| 5 | Region 2, middle | The story's largest set piece |
| 6 | Region 2, end | A betrayal or reversal |
| 7 | Region 3, early | The offer |
| 8 | Region 3, middle | The reveal |
| 9 | Region 3, late | The night before |
| 10 | Before the Champion | The climax |

5. **Threads.** Each event keeps three to five hidden counters. Beats read them; endings are decided by them. The player is never shown a number, only people behaving differently.
6. **Outside hooks.** Each event lists ordinary choices elsewhere in the run that move its Threads: how the player treats a certain type, whether they released a Pokémon, whether they lied in an unrelated event.
7. **Nothing here ends a run.** Losing a beat's battle changes the story. Losing the climax gives the worst ending, and the Champion battle still happens, in a different world.
8. **The Champion changes.** Every ending alters the final battle: the arena, the Champion's opening line, sometimes the Champion.
9. **Title cards.** Each beat opens on a black screen with its title in white serif capitals, held for two seconds in silence.
10. **They can be switched off,** together or one by one, in settings.

## 2. GUIDANCE OF GIRATINA

### 2.1 What it is

A cult called the **Distortion Division** means to raise the Distorted One and let it rule the overworld. They are certain they are right. They will use anyone to do it: adults, children, Pokémon, each other. The player is confronted, again and again, with choices that have no clean answer. It draws on the shape of scripture (a sacrifice demanded, doors marked, a flood, a betrayal by a friend, a tempter in the wilderness) and on the cold, institutional dread of stories about adults who have decided the world should end and have filled in the paperwork.

### 2.2 The Threads

| Thread | Range | What it measures | Raised by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Faith** | 0–10 | How far the Division believes the player is theirs | Listening, offering, accepting gifts |
| **Doubt** | 0–10 | How many of the Division the player has shaken | Using names, sparing, asking questions |
| **Curse** | 0–5 | The old man's curse, if he spoke it | Leaving; defying; each death the player causes |
| **Witnesses** | 0–6 | Who stands with the player at the end | Trusting Looker, Cynthia, Rowan, N, the rival; one more is a secret |
| **The Lost** | a name | The Pokémon the player gave up, if any | Beat 3 |

### 2.3 Outside hooks

| Elsewhere in the run, the player | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Catches a Ghost-type before Beat 2 | The old man's followers nod to them in the street. Faith +1. |
| Releases any Pokémon | The Division finds it. It is in the Choir's care by Beat 5. |
| Lies in any unrelated event | Curse +1. The old man said misfortune; this is what it looks like. |
| Completes *The Shedinja Question* or *The Last Stop* | Doubt +1: the player has seen what a kind afterlife looks like, and says so. |
| Stands with an evil team | The Division approaches that team; Ghetsis or Cyrus arrives a beat early. |
| Holds The Renegade blessing | The Division already knows the player's name. Faith starts at 2. |
| Has a Giratina in the party at Beat 8 | The reveal plays differently, and worse. |
| Never loses a Pokémon in the run | The Avatar notices, and wants to know what the player is afraid of. |

### 2.4 The Division

- **The Faithful.** Ordinary members. Grey robes, a handprint in ash on the chest. Most joined after a loss. They are polite. They give directions and share food.
- **Deacons.** They carry out the rites. Each has a name, and their name is the most dangerous thing the player can learn about them.
- **The Inquisitor.** Hunts heretics. Who this is depends on Beat 3.
- **The Choir.** Children taken from houses that would not mark their doors. They are fed and clothed and taught the litany, and they sing it beautifully. Nothing is done to them on screen. What has been done is plain from how quiet they are.
- **The Synod.** Seven veiled chairs. Six voices. The seventh chair has never been heard to speak, and the others stop when it leans forward.
- **The Avatar of Distant Death.** Section 2.7.

**Their litany** (heard in every beat, a little more of it each time):

> *What was cast out shall be called home.*
> *What was divided shall be folded.*
> *There is no far. There is no after.*
> *O praise be the Distortion.*

### 2.5 The beats

#### Beat 1 — THE OLD MAN AT THE CROSSROADS *(Stage 2, forced)*

An old man steps into the road. He asks whether the player has heard of the Distorted One. He does not wait for an answer. He never names a Pokémon.

> "In the beginning there was the First, and the First made three. One to keep the hours. One to keep the distances. And one to keep what the other two could not bear to look at.
> The third was strong, and it was honest, and for its honesty they called it violent and put it out of the world. They built this"—he taps the road with his stick—"on top of the door.
> It is not dead, child. It is *underneath*. It has been listening to us walk about on its ceiling for a very long time."

Two boxes fill the screen: **LISTEN** and **LEAVE**.

- **LISTEN.** He tells why he loves it. He lost someone. The priests of the other three told him she had gone *far* and it would be a *long time*. Only the Distorted One, he says, promises a place with no far and no after. He presses a grey stone into the player's hand. "O praise be the Distortion." He walks away. *Faith 2. The stone is a key item, and it matters in Beat 3.*
- **LEAVE.** His hand closes on the player's shoulder, much stronger than it should be.

> "Heretic. You walk on its roof and will not hear its name. Then hear mine for you. May what you love go first. May your road be long and lead back here. May you be the last of your company standing, and may you understand, then, what I was offering."

  He lets go. *Curse 2.* For the rest of the run, the **Curse** shows itself in small, fair ways: once per stage per point, something goes slightly wrong (an item is found broken, a wild Pokémon flees, an opponent's first critical hit lands). It never decides a battle. It is there to be felt.

#### Beat 2 — THE MARKED DOORS

A town where half the doors carry a grey handprint. Marked houses are left alone. Unmarked houses are visited at night for a **tithe**: one Pokémon. A house with no Pokémon and a child loses the child to the Choir.

A family asks the player to mark their door for them. They cannot bring themselves to do it.

- **Mark it:** they are safe. Faith +1. The player's hand stays grey for a stage.
- **Wipe the marks off every door:** the Division comes that night for the whole street; a battle to defend it. Win, and the town is free and afraid. Lose, and the tithe is doubled.
- **Stand guard at one unmarked door:** a Deacon comes alone. He is tired. If the player asks his name, he gives it, and looks startled to have been asked. *Doubt +1. The name is recorded.*

#### Beat 3 — THE OFFERING

A rift has opened in a hillside: a wound in the air, with the wrong sky behind it. A Deacon stands before it. Bound at the edge is the **Pokémon the player obtained most recently**.

> "It must be given a life. Yours is not asked for; you are a guest. So choose. The one who came to you last. Or me."

The Deacon means it. They are not afraid. They believe they are going home.

- **THE POKÉMON.** It is taken. It looks at the player the whole way. It is gone from the party, and its name becomes **The Lost**. The party's Bond falls. The rival, when they hear, does not speak to the player for a stage.
- **THE DEACON.** They thank the player, turn, and walk into the rift.

  > The rift does not take them the way a door takes someone. It takes them the way a hand closes. Their feet leave the ground and do not rise: *down* has moved, for them alone, and they fall sideways into the air and stop there, held. For a moment they are smiling. Then the space they are in begins to be smaller than they are. The robe goes first, drawn inward at every edge as if into a drain. The arms fold where arms have no joint. There is a sound like green wood being wrung. They do not scream, because by then what would scream has been turned to face the other way, and what is left is a shape being pressed through a gap the width of a coin, wet and patient, a little at a time. The last thing through is the hand, open, palm out. The ash print on it is the same size as the one on the door in the town. Then the air is flat and clean, and something on the far side of it, very large, makes a noise of contentment.

  *Faith +2:* the Division counts this as the player's offering. *Curse +1.* The player's Pokémon saw it; the one that was bound will not leave its Ball for a stage. The Deacon is declared a martyr, and someone who loved them becomes **the Inquisitor**.
- **The third answer** exists only if the player can do one of three things: say the Deacon's **name** (learned in Beat 2); hold out **the old man's stone**; or have **Doubt 2**. Then a third box appears: **NEITHER.**
  The Deacon hesitates. The rift, offered nothing, does not close. It stays in the hillside for the rest of the run, and the region around it sickens (a world-state penalty). Nobody dies. The Deacon is taken away by the Division for failing. *Doubt +2.* They can be found again in Beat 6.

#### Beat 4 — THE INVESTIGATOR

**Looker** has been following disappearances across three regions and has a wall of string to show for it. **Professor Rowan** has been sent the Division's scripture and does not like how much of it is accurate. **Cynthia** is at a ruin nearby, reading an inscription over and over; she recognises the handwriting in the Division's oldest text, and will not yet say whose.

The player chooses how much to tell them. Each one trusted is a **Witness**. Each has a price: Looker wants the player to wear a wire into Beat 5. Rowan wants the stone. Cynthia wants to come along.

#### Beat 5 — THE BAPTISM

A lake town. The water has turned over: it reflects a sky that is not above it. The Division is leading people into it in rows. Those who go under come out **quiet**: calm, smiling, hollow. The Choir sings from the bank. If the player released a Pokémon this run, it is here, sitting with them.

Two other groups have come to watch. Remnants of **Team Galactic** see their old leader's dream offered by someone else: a world without spirit. **Team Plasma** has split: **Ghetsis** thinks the Division is a tool he can pick up; **N** is on his knees at the waterline with his hands over his ears, because he can hear what the Pokémon under the water are saying.

- **Break it up by force.** A wide battle against Deacons. People panic; some run into the water. Fewer are saved than the player hoped. *Curse +1.*
- **Go under.** The player walks in to pull one person out: someone from Beat 2. Underwater there is no battle, only the litany and a choice of which hand to take. They come out with one person, and with something of the lake in their eyes for a stage. *Faith +1, Doubt +1.*
- **Call them by name.** Only if the player has learned names. Each name spoken aloud turns one of the quiet around in the water. *Doubt +1 per name.*
- **Help N.** He tells the player what the Pokémon are saying. It is one word, over and over, and it is not "home".

#### Beat 6 — THE KISS

Someone gives the player up. Who depends on the run:

| Condition | The betrayer |
| --- | --- |
| The player accepted Ghetsis's help | Ghetsis, for a seat at the Synod |
| Faith is 6 or more | Looker's wire is found, and it is the *player* who is asked to hand Looker over |
| The Deacon was spared in Beat 3 | The Deacon betrays the Division instead, and brings the player the Synod's meeting place |
| None of these | A Witness, under threat to someone they love |

The player is brought before **the Synod**: seven veiled chairs in a hall with no visible walls. Six voices discuss the player as if reading minutes. The seventh chair leans forward. It is the first time the **Avatar** speaks.

> "Leave them. I would like to see what it does."

#### Beat 7 — THE WILDERNESS

The Avatar comes to the player alone, on an empty stretch of road, with no guard. He is courteous. He has clearly looked forward to this. He offers three things, one at a time, and each is real.

| The offer | What it is | The hidden cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **"Command that it be returned."** | The Lost comes back, now. (If nobody was lost: any one Pokémon the player has ever released.) | It comes back *through* the Distortion. It fights for the other side in the climax until called by name. |
| **"All of this could be yours."** | The Champion's seat, without the battle. | Accepting ends the run's Champion fight and gives the Usurper ending. |
| **"Step off. It will bear you up."** | The player's Pokémon can no longer faint; they are left at 1 HP instead, once each per battle. | Each time it triggers, Faith +1, and the Pokémon is a little quieter afterwards. |

Refusing all three is possible. He is not angry. He is interested.

> "Mere mortals always imagine that refusing is the brave thing. It is only the slow thing. You will come to the same door. I will hold it."

#### Beat 8 — THE REVEAL

At a spring where the water stands in a column, the player meets Giratina. It can be battled. It can be caught.

Then Cynthia, or Rowan, or the old man himself if nobody else is left, explains what the Division's oldest text says, and whose hand wrote it.

- The text was written long ago by a man named **Volo**, who spent his life trying to reach the First through the Distorted One, and failed, and wrote down everything he learned so that someone else would not.
- The Avatar found the text. He believes he is Volo, returned. He is not. Cynthia is certain, and will not say how she knows.
- And the last page: **every Giratina anyone has ever seen, fought, or caught is an avatar.** A finger pushed through a curtain. When the First sealed the Distorted One away, it did not stay a creature in a place. Over ages it sank into the place and the place sank into it. **The true Giratina is the Distortion World itself:** a whole world's worth of hatred and patience, shaped like nothing, wanting in.

If the player caught the Giratina a minute ago: Cynthia looks at the Ball. "That is a finger. You have caught its finger. It let you."
If a Giratina is already in the party: it turns its head and looks at the player, and for one second its eyes are not its own.

#### Beat 9 — THE NIGHT BEFORE

A camp below the League. Whoever the player gathered is here. Each Witness offers one thing for tomorrow and asks one question tonight. The answers are remembered.

- **Looker** asks if the player is afraid. He is.
- **Cynthia** says who Volo was to her family. She will hold the line with the player.
- **N** asks whether a Pokémon can forgive. He means the Lost.
- **Rowan** returns the stone, if he took it. He has carved a second word under the first.
- **The rival** says whatever their personality lets them say. It is not much. It is enough.
- **The secret Witness** is **Cyrus**, and he appears only if the player has never once lied to anyone in this story. He has been to the place the Division wants to open. He went there *wanting* a world without spirit. He says, flatly, that it is not empty. That is the problem with it.

The last conversation is with the party. If there is a Lost, its place by the fire is left open, and the others have left it open.

#### Beat 10 — THE RAISING *(before the Champion)*

At the top of the League's steps the sky has been opened like the hillside was, and far larger.

**Phase 1 — The Faithful.** The Inquisitor and the Synod's Deacons. For every point of Doubt, one opponent lowers their Poké Ball and steps aside before the battle. At Doubt 8 or more there is no Phase 1.

**Phase 2 — The Avatar.** His team is Ghost and Dragon, led by a Giratina he calls his own body. If the player accepted the first offer, the Lost is on his side, hollow-eyed. Saying its name costs a turn and brings it back. When his last Pokémon falls, the player is asked one thing: **STRIKE** or **LOWER YOUR HAND**.

**Phase 3 — The Distorted One.** Not a Pokémon on the field. The **arena**. The floor becomes the wall. Type matchups invert, then right themselves, then invert. There is nothing to knock out. The player must **survive ten turns** and perform three acts, each made possible, or impossible, by something done long before:

| The act | Needs |
| --- | --- |
| **Close the doors** | The marks were wiped, or one door was guarded, in Beat 2 |
| **Call the quiet** | At least three names, or Doubt 5 |
| **Give back what was offered** | The old man's stone, or the Lost returned, or the unclosed rift from Beat 3 sealed with a Witness's help |

Each act done shortens the ten turns by three. With none done, the ten turns must be endured in full.

### 2.6 The reveal, stated plainly for the builder

- Giratina the Pokémon remains catchable, ordinary and legal. Nothing about it changes mechanically.
- The story's antagonist is never a catchable thing. It has no sprite, no HP bar and no cry. It is shown only as what it does to rooms.
- This is why the Renegade blessing's Communion is "the mirror stage" and not "the true Giratina obeys you". Nobody gets that.

### 2.7 The Avatar of Distant Death

- **No name.** Nobody in the Division has one for him. The title is what the Synod calls the chair.
- **What he is like:** soft-spoken, unhurried, immaculate. He sits with his hands folded in front of his mouth. He never raises his voice until the end. He is fond of the player in the way a person is fond of an experiment going well.
- **What he believes:** that he is Volo reborn, the Distorted One's chosen body; that he is divine; that humans are "mere mortals" (he says it gently, the way one says "children"); that every cruelty the Division commits is a kindness the victims are too small to see. He has never, for one second, considered that he might be wrong.
- **What he actually is:** a man. Clever, bereaved long ago, and so unable to bear being ordinary that he built a religion to avoid it.
- **How he fights:** at the top AI tier. He sacrifices his own Pokémon without a flicker.
- **His tell:** he is the only person in the story who never uses anyone's name.

### 2.8 The endings

| Ending | How | What happens | The Champion battle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Sealed** | All three acts done | The sky closes. The breakdown (2.9). | Normal arena; the Champion has heard, and opens by thanking the player |
| **The One Who Stayed** | One or two acts done; or none, and the ten turns survived | The door will not shut from this side. The Witness with the strongest bond holds it from within. If there are no Witnesses, the player's highest-Bond Pokémon does. | The Champion fights with one empty slot, in respect |
| **Mercy** | Sealed, with Doubt 8+, and the player lowered their hand | The Division walks away in ones and twos. The old man is at the crossroads. He asks if the player will listen now. This time he tells them his wife's name. | The Champion's crowd includes people in grey, with their hoods down |
| **Raised** | The party is wiped in any phase of the climax | It comes through. Not all of it. Enough. | The Champion battle is fought in a world turned over: inverted matchups, and a Champion who is trying not to look up |
| **The Convert** | Faith 8+ and the player chooses to complete the rite | The player raises it. Every Pokémon in the party gains a Distortion form: stronger, and quiet. | The Champion is the last person in the region who is not, and the fight is for that |
| **The Usurper** | The second offer was accepted | The player takes the seventh chair. The Avatar kneels, delighted. | None. The run ends on the Hall of Fame screen, with six veiled figures where the team should be |

*The run is never made unwinnable. Raised, Convert and Usurper are endings, and are recorded as completed runs with their own marks in the archive.*

### 2.9 The breakdown

Only in **Sealed** and **Mercy**. The sky has closed. His Giratina has gone back into its Ball by itself. He is standing alone at the top of the steps and nothing is happening. This is the first time his voice rises.

> "No. *No.* Look again. You have not understood what you are looking at.
> I am not a man who failed. I am the will of a god, and a god does not *fail*: the world fails Him. You failed. This ground failed. The sky failed, it closed, it had *no right* to close.
> I was chosen. Before any of you drew breath I was chosen. I have died once already for this and I came back, I came *back*, do you know what it costs to come back? I gave it everything. I gave it everyone. I gave it people who loved me, and it *took* them, so do not tell me it was not listening!
> You are dust that learned to stand upright. You are an afternoon. I was going to make you *eternal* and you chose to stay small, small, crawling about on a ceiling. And you look at me. You dare. With *pity*.
> I am not one of you. I am not one of you. I am not"

He stops, because he has heard his own voice echo off the stone, and it is only a voice.

- In **Sealed**, the last of the rift, a crack no wider than a coin, takes him by the hand. He has time to understand that he is not being welcomed.
- In **Mercy**, nothing takes him. He is left standing there, ordinary, in the morning. Looker puts a coat round his shoulders. That is worse, and it is the better ending.

## 3. THE WEATHER WAR

- **Legend:** Kyogre, Groudon, Rayquaza. **Cast:** Archie, Maxie, Steven, Wallace, a meteorologist nobody listens to.
- **Tone:** a slow disaster and an argument between two people who are each half right.
- **Opening (Stage 2):** a woman on a harbour wall is measuring the tide with a stick. It is four fingers higher than last year. Boxes: **HELP HER MEASURE** / **WALK ON**.
- **Threads:** *Sea* and *Land* (the two rise against each other), *Trust* (whether anyone believes the measurements), *The Sky* (whether the third one has been woken).
- **How it runs:** every region's map changes with the Threads. At high Sea, coastal nodes are underwater and new sea routes open. At high Land, rivers are dry beds and desert Pokémon walk through towns. Archie and Maxie each ask the player for small, reasonable favours, and each favour moves the world a little.
- **Outside hooks:** which starter type the player leads with; every event that involves water or drought (*The Stranding*, *The Drought Ranch*); either half of the Quarrel as the run's blessing.
- **The dilemma (Beat 3):** a dam. Open it and a valley town floods to save a city. Keep it shut and the city's reservoir fails. There is a third answer, and it needs both leaders in one room.
- **Climax:** both wake. The battle is fought on a map that floods and dries turn by turn; the player's job is to keep *both* from winning until the sky answers, or to choose a side and live in that world.
- **Endings:** *Balance* (both sleep; the two leaders build something together); *The Drowned World* and *The Dry World* (playable Champion battles on changed arenas); *The Long Storm* (neither sleeps; the Champion battle is fought in every weather at once).

## 4. THE MADE ONE

- **Legend:** Mewtwo. **Cast:** a geneticist who has stopped sleeping, Giovanni, Blaine, a child who is not quite there.
- **Tone:** quiet and sad. Almost no villains.
- **Opening (Stage 2):** a Pokémon the player does not recognise is sitting by the road in a torn cloak, looking at its own hands. It asks one question, directly into the player's head: *What are you for?* Boxes: **ANSWER** / **SAY NOTHING**.
- **Threads:** *Purpose* (the answers the player has given it), *Kindness* (how the player treats their own Pokémon, which it is watching), *The Leash* (how close its makers are to taking it back).
- **How it runs:** it follows at a distance for the whole run and asks one question per region. It watches how the player handles fainting, releasing, evolving. It copies what it sees: a player who uses Pokémon as tools is teaching it that Pokémon are tools.
- **Outside hooks:** every release; every evolution forced or refused (*The Pokémon That Wouldn't Evolve*); whether the player ever sacrifices one Pokémon to bring another in safely.
- **The dilemma (Beat 3):** its maker begs the player to return it "before it understands what was done to it". It is standing right there.
- **Climax:** its makers arrive with a way to switch it off. It asks the player the first question again. The battle is against whoever the player's answers have turned it against: its makers, the player, or itself.
- **Endings:** *It Chooses* (it leaves, free, and nods once); *It Stays* (it joins, and never asks again); *It Obeys* (it is taken back, and the Champion has a new Pokémon); *It Decides Against Us* (the hardest fight in the game, and it is right to be angry).

## 5. THE SEVEN DAYS

- **Legend:** Jirachi. **Cast:** a travelling astronomer, a town with a festival, the player's rival.
- **Tone:** a fable. Warm, then bittersweet.
- **Opening (Stage 2):** a comet is overhead and a child hands the player a paper tag. "You write a wish on it. You only get one." Boxes: **WRITE ONE** / **KEEP IT BLANK**.
- **Threads:** *Wishes Made* (up to seven across the run), *Cost* (who paid for each), *Days Left*.
- **How it runs:** Jirachi is awake for seven days, and the run is those days. Seven times, something offers to grant a wish, a real one with a real effect: a Pokémon restored, a battle skipped, a rival's Lead reset. Each wish is granted **out of someone else's**: the player sees, later, the person whose wish did not come true so theirs could.
- **Outside hooks:** every reward the player declines; every promise kept; Jirachi's own Promise, if it is the run's Guest.
- **The dilemma (Beat 3):** two people have wished for opposite things, and the player is holding the tag that decides it.
- **Climax:** the seventh night. There is one wish left in the world and everybody the player met is on the hill. Jirachi asks the player who should have it.
- **Endings:** *The Blank Tag* (the player made no wishes at all, and is given the only one that costs nobody anything); *Given Away* (the last wish goes to someone else; the player learns whose it was a region later); *Seven for One* (the player took all seven; they get everything, and the hill is empty); *Goodnight* (Jirachi sleeps; the player may ask it to remember them, which is not a wish, and it does).

## 6. THE WEAPON AND THE FLOWER

- **Legend:** the ultimate weapon; Xerneas and Yveltal behind it. **Cast:** a very tall, very old wanderer; Lysandre; Diantha; Professor Sycamore; a Floette.
- **Tone:** grief on the scale of history.
- **Opening (Stage 2):** a giant of a man is sitting under a tree far too small for him. He asks if the player has seen a Floette with a red flower. He has been asking for three thousand years. Boxes: **SIT WITH HIM** / **KEEP WALKING**.
- **Threads:** *Grief* (his), *Beauty* (Lysandre's argument, and how much of it the player has agreed with), *The Key* (who holds it), *The Flower* (whether she can be found).
- **How it runs:** the old man walks the same road as the player, always a little behind. He tells the story in pieces: a war, a Pokémon he loved, a machine he built to bring her back, and what he then did with the machine. Lysandre has found it. He is not a cultist. He is a man who loves the world and has concluded it cannot be shared.
- **Outside hooks:** whether the player has ever revived anything at a cost; *The Ones You Left*; the Rainbow and the Tree and the Cocoon blessings.
- **The dilemma (Beat 3):** a Pokémon of the player's is lost in this story, and the old man tells them, without being asked, that the machine could return it. He tells them what the machine eats.
- **Climax:** the weapon is charged. Lysandre offers the player the seat beside him in the world that comes after. The old man asks for the key. The player is holding it.
- **Endings:** *Buried* (the weapon is broken and the Floette comes back by her own choice, which is the only way she ever would); *Fired Once More* (the player uses it to bring one life back, and the game shows, in full, what it took); *The Beautiful World* (Lysandre wins; the Champion battle takes place in a silent, perfect garden); *Still Walking* (nobody wins; the old man goes on down the road, and the player may walk with him a while).

## 7. Building them

- **Order:** the framework; then *Guidance of Giratina* Beats 1, 3 and 10 with a single ending, to prove the Threads work; then the rest of its beats; then one of the gentler events (*The Seven Days*) before the other three, so the bag holds two tones early.
- **Text volume:** *Guidance of Giratina* as written needs about 10 scenes, 40 branch variants and 6 endings. The others are specified here at outline depth and need the same treatment.
- **Tests:** the event is drawn in 35% of runs; all five are seen before a repeat; every beat can be reached with every combination of earlier choices; no combination of Threads leaves the climax without an ending; no ending blocks the Champion battle except The Usurper, which ends the run as a completed one.
