# GREENNEEDLE — NEW EVENTS

**Adds to:** your `Greenneedle.md` list and the "extras" notes. Nothing here replaces an event of yours.
**Count:** 38 new events, written to your format and a little longer: what happens, what the player can do, what each choice leads to, the reward, and what comes back later.
*Every event, ability and move named here is original. Numbers are starting values for tuning. Two of your newer rules are respected throughout: no healing items are used in battle, and nothing here ends a run.*

---

## How to read an entry

Each event carries one or more of your classifications (Miniature Episode, Region-Wide Story, Companion Story, Pseudo Stage, Interrupting Episode, Mystery Episode, Rival Episode, Tournament Episode, Community Episode, Reunion Episode, World Event, Legendary Episode, Hub Destination, Finale Episode), then five parts:

- **Cold open:** what the player sees before they understand it.
- **The episode:** what happens, and what the player can do.
- **Endings:** each choice and what it leads to.
- **Reward:** including any new ability or move, with its limit.
- **Afterstory:** how it comes back.

**Three rules for every new ability and move below.** Each is announced the first time it triggers. Each has a stated limit (once per battle, once per switch-in, a cap). None raises damage by more than 20%.

## A. Companion stories

### 1. The Eevee Who Couldn't Choose
*(Region-Wide Story, Companion Story)*

- **Cold open:** An Eevee is sitting in the middle of a crossroads with seven roads, turning in a slow circle.
- **The episode:** It follows the player, at a distance, for the whole region. At each stage one admirer appears and tries to win it over with a gift: a fisherman with a Water Stone, a street magician with a Fire Stone, an electrician, a night-shift nurse, a gardener, a mountain guide, a ribbon-maker. The Eevee looks at the player every time. The player may nod, shake their head, or shrug.
- **Endings:** The game counts what the player *did* across the region, not what they answered. Most battles won at night: it leans toward Umbreon. Most Pokémon healed and none fainted: Sylveon. Most routes through water: Vaporeon. And so on for all eight. At the region's last rest it either evolves in a cutscene, into the form the journey earned, or, if the player shrugged every time, it sits down next to them and stays an Eevee.
- **Reward:** The Eevee or its evolution joins. An Eevee that never evolved has **Open Road**: the first move it uses each time it enters battle gains same-type bonus, whatever its type. Once per switch-in.
- **Afterstory:** Each admirer whose gift was refused is met once in a later region, doing fine, with a different partner. The ribbon-maker has made one for the Eevee anyway.

### 2. The Dog at the Station
*(Miniature Episode that becomes a Region-Wide Story)*

- **Cold open:** A Growlithe is sitting on a railway platform, facing the tracks. A station clerk puts down a bowl without looking at it. He has clearly done this for a long time.
- **The episode:** Its trainer left on a train some years ago and did not come back. It meets every arrival. The player can sit with it, feed it, try to lead it away, or ask around town. Asking reveals three different stories about where the trainer went. One is true.
- **Endings:** **Find the truth** (three short errands across three stages): the trainer is alive, ill, and too ashamed to send for it. The player can carry a letter, or carry the Growlithe. **Sit with it** at every visit and do nothing else: on the last visit it gets up, walks to the player, and looks back at the tracks once. **Lead it away** early: it follows for one node and then runs back.
- **Reward:** Reunited: a *Fire Stone* from the trainer and a line in the Notebook. If it chose the player: a Growlithe with **Vigil**: if it has been on the field for three full turns, the next hit it takes this battle does 50% less. Once per battle.
- **Afterstory:** If reunited, the clerk is met in the next region, on holiday, telling the story badly to strangers.

### 3. The Ball That Wouldn't Open
*(Mystery Episode, Companion Story, Region-Wide Story)*

- **Cold open:** An old Poké Ball in the mud, scuffed, with a strip of tape across it. Written on the tape in a child's hand: *sorry*.
- **The episode:** The Pokémon inside will not come out. It takes no party slot. At each rest the player may talk to it, leave it by the fire, hold it during a battle, or put it away. It reacts in small ways: the Ball is warm after a win, it rolls toward the player's hurt Pokémon, it rocks once when the player loses.
- **Endings:** After five kind acts it opens by itself, at a rest, when nobody is watching: the player finds it sitting among the party as if it had always been there. Its species is drawn from the region's rarer pool. If the player tries to **force** the Ball (a shop offers to), it opens, the Pokémon bolts, and is met again much later as a wild Pokémon that will not be caught.
- **Reward:** The Pokémon, with **Trust Earned**: it cannot be made to flinch, be confused or be infatuated while its trainer has at least one other Pokémon able to battle.
- **Afterstory:** In the next region a young trainer recognises it. The player decides whether the child gets to say the word on the tape out loud.

### 4. The Tyrogue Who Wanted All Three
*(Companion Story)*

- **Cold open:** A Tyrogue is running between three dojos on one street, bowing at each door and being thrown out of each for training at the other two.
- **The episode:** It asks to travel with the player for a stage. In each battle it takes part in, the game tracks how it won: kicks, punches, or staying on its feet. The three dojo masters turn up one at a time to watch and to insult each other.
- **Endings:** A clear lead in one style evolves it into that form at the stage's end. An even split does something the masters say is impossible: it does not evolve, and all three bow.
- **Reward:** A Hitmonlee, Hitmonchan or Hitmontop with one tutor move from its dojo; or an unevolved Tyrogue with the move **Three Schools**: 70 power, Fighting, and it uses whichever of Attack, Defense or Speed is currently highest for its damage. 
- **Afterstory:** The three dojos merge. The sign has three names and is repainted each time a master wins an argument.

### 5. The Egg That Listened
*(Region-Wide Story, Companion Story)*

- **Cold open:** A day-care worker presses an egg into the player's hands. "Nobody's claimed it. Talk to it. They hear more than you'd think."
- **The episode:** The egg rides along for a region. It "hears" everything: which move the player uses most, whether they switch out hurt Pokémon or leave them in, how they answer in events. Now and then it rocks, and the game shows a single word: *loud*, *careful*, *again*.
- **Endings:** It hatches at the region's last rest into a baby Pokémon from the current region's pool. Its nature reflects the player's habits (aggressive play gives a bold or hasty one; careful play, a calm one). It knows one move no baby should know: the move the player used most often that region, if it can legally hold it, or the nearest move of that type if not.
- **Reward:** The hatchling, with Bond already at Friendly.
- **Afterstory:** The first time it sees that move used by the Pokémon it learned it from, it tries to copy the stance.

### 6. The Duelist
*(Region-Wide Story, Rival Episode)*

- **Cold open:** A Farfetch'd plants its leek in the road like a sword and points it at one of the player's Pokémon. Not the strongest. The one that last landed a critical hit.
- **The episode:** It has chosen a rival, and it is not the player. Once per stage it returns to duel that one Pokémon, one on one, no switching. It bows before and after. It gets better each time, and it studies: after a loss it carries an answer to the move that beat it.
- **Endings:** Best of five across the region. If the player's Pokémon wins the series, the Duelist kneels and offers its leek. If the Duelist wins, it bows and leaves, and the player's Pokémon stares after it for a node. If the player refuses a duel, it waits; it never ambushes.
- **Reward:** Winning the series: the Duelist joins, or (the player's choice) leaves its leek with the Pokémon it respected. That Pokémon gains **Worthy Opponent**: +1 to its highest stat on entry when the foe's active Pokémon is at full HP.
- **Afterstory:** A Duelist that left returns in a later region as a Sirfetch'd, in the crowd at the player's hardest fight. It nods once.

### 7. The Shedinja Question
*(Mystery Episode, Companion Story)*

- **Cold open:** A Nincada the player has not caught evolves in the grass ahead and flies off. Its shell stays where it was. Then the shell gets up.
- **The episode:** The Shedinja follows the player because nobody else is there. It does not battle. At three rests it does one thing: it hovers in front of another Pokémon and holds still, as if waiting to be told what it is. The player chooses who answers: a party Pokémon, the player, or nobody.
- **Endings:** If the player's Pokémon accept it (three rests, three different Pokémon), it joins as itself. If the player sets out to find the Ninjask it came from, the two meet and neither recognises the other; the Shedinja goes on with the player anyway. If nobody ever answers, it is gone one morning.
- **Reward:** A Shedinja with **Second Self**: the first time it would be knocked out in a battle, it survives, and the attacker's move is revealed to the player for the rest of the battle. Once per battle.
- **Afterstory:** It turns up in the background of every later Ghost-type event, slightly apart from the others.

### 8. The Understudy
*(Companion Story, Community Episode)*

- **Cold open:** Backstage at a small theatre. A Mime Jr. is copying the star Mr. Mime's act, perfectly, to an audience of mops.
- **The episode:** The star has not come in tonight. The director needs someone on stage in an hour. The player can coach Mime Jr. (a three-step imitation game), go and look for the star, or take the stage themselves, which goes as badly as it sounds.
- **Endings:** Mime Jr. performs: nervous, then wonderful. The star is found asleep in the costume cupboard, and watches the last minute from the wings with an unreadable face. Afterwards it either hands over its bow tie or walks out; that depends on whether the player went to look for it first.
- **Reward:** Mime Jr. asks to see the world: it joins with **Curtain Call**: when it switches out, the Pokémon replacing it copies the last stat boost Mime Jr. had. One stage, once per battle.
- **Afterstory:** A poster for the theatre appears in the next region. Whose face is on it depends on the ending.

## B. Mysteries and the strange

### 9. The Last Stop
*(Mystery Episode, Pseudo Stage)*

- **Cold open:** A railway platform that is not on the stage map. The clock has no hands. A Drifloon is holding a lantern.
- **The episode:** A train arrives, full of Ghost-types and things that are not quite passengers. The player may board. Three carriages: a dining car where a Gengar deals cards for memories (bet a move, win a different one), a sleeper where something is under every blanket, and a mail car full of undelivered letters, one of which is addressed to a Pokémon in the player's party, from someone it knew before.
- **Endings:** Ride to the end: the conductor (a Dusknoir) punches the player's ticket and returns it. The date on it is in the future. Get off early: nothing follows. Read the letter aloud: that Pokémon's Bond rises by two, and it is quiet for a node.
- **Reward:** A Drifloon that carried the lantern, with the move **Last Stop**: 60 power, Ghost; if it knocks out its target, the user switches out for free.
- **Afterstory:** The ticket stays in the bag. On the stage whose number matches the date, the platform is there again, and the train is waiting. Nobody on it will say why.

### 10. The Mirror Lake
*(Mystery Episode)*

- **Cold open:** A lake so still that the player's reflection is a half-second late.
- **The episode:** The reflections climb out: the player's own team as it was at the *last battle the player lost or nearly lost*, with the moves used in it, played by the AI the way the player played. It is the only battle in the game against yourself.
- **Endings:** Win: the reflections bow and go back in. Lose: they go back in anyway, and nothing is taken. Refuse to fight and skip stones instead: one reflection stays on the bank and watches the party leave.
- **Reward:** A **Reflection Shard** (held item): the first time the holder is hit by a move it also knows, it takes half damage. Once per battle.
- **Afterstory:** The reflection that stayed is seen twice more, in water, a half-second late.

### 11. The Night Parade
*(World Event, Mystery Episode)*

- **Cold open:** Once per region, on a night node: every street lamp goes out in a line, coming toward the player.
- **The episode:** A procession of a hundred Ghost- and Dark-types passes through, carrying lanterns, masks and a palanquin with its curtains shut. The player can **hide**, **watch**, or **join the back of the line**. Joining leads to the Spirit Market at the parade's end, where nothing is sold for money: stalls trade a move for a move, a memory for an item, a nickname for a secret.
- **Endings:** Hide: safe, and a mask is found on the road next morning. Watch: the palanquin's curtain moves, and one Pokémon in the party gains a point of Bond out of fright. Join: the market, and the player must leave before the lanterns burn down (six actions) or wake up at the next node with one item swapped for another.
- **Reward:** By trade. The best stall offers any one egg move for a move the Pokémon already knows.
- **Afterstory:** Whatever was in the palanquin is revealed only in the game's last region, and only to players who joined every parade.

### 12. The Cartographer
*(Mystery Episode, utility)*

- **Cold open:** A Xatu on a rock, staring at the sun, with a hundred hand-drawn maps pinned under stones around it.
- **The episode:** Each map is of a stage. One is of this stage, and it is finished: every node, including the ones the player has not reached. The Xatu will let the player look, for a price. It does not want money. It wants the player to hear one true thing about how their run ends.
- **Endings:** **Look and listen:** the whole stage map is revealed, and the Xatu shows a single image from the run's future (a place, a silhouette, a number). The image is generated from the run's seed and will come true. **Look and refuse to listen:** the map blurs after ten seconds. **Leave:** nothing.
- **Reward:** Knowledge. If the prophecy has come true by the end of the run, a Natu is waiting at the final rest.
- **Afterstory:** The prophecy can be *changed* by exactly one kind of choice: a Legendary Blessing's Communion power. The Xatu is furious, and impressed.

### 13. The Ones You Left
*(Reunion Episode, Mystery Episode; needs a previous lost run on this save)*

- **Cold open:** An abandoned camp: a cold fire, a bedroll, six Poké Balls in a row on a log, and a trainer's notebook. The handwriting is the player's.
- **The episode:** It is the camp of the player's own last failed run. The notebook lists that team by nickname. The Balls open: the Pokémon inside have been waiting. They are at the level they were when the run ended, scaled to the current stage, and they remember the last battle.
- **Endings:** The player may take **one** with them. The other five decide for themselves: some go with a passing trainer, some stay at the camp, one always walks into the woods. Taking none is allowed; the player can bury the notebook instead, and the party gains a small permanent Bond bonus from watching.
- **Reward:** One Pokémon from a past run, with a visible mark on its summary (*Came Back*) and one move it knew then.
- **Afterstory:** The five that were not taken appear in the *Final Reunion*. The one that walked into the woods is the last to arrive.

### 14. The Arcade Cabinet
*(Mystery Episode, minigame)*

- **Cold open:** An unplugged arcade machine in the back of a shop is running. The high-score table has one entry, and the initials are "HELP".
- **The episode:** A Porygon has been stuck inside for years. The player plays three short games on the cabinet (a maze, a block-stacker, a one-screen shooter). The Porygon is the player's character in each one, and plays slightly differently depending on how it is treated: rushed, it panics; given time, it finds shortcuts.
- **Endings:** Clear all three: it steps out of the screen. Fail one: it pushes the player's last life back at them and waits. The shop owner offers money for the cabinet "as is"; selling it ends the event and is remembered.
- **Reward:** A Porygon with **Debug**: once per battle, when it is hit by a move for the second time, that move's secondary effect fails.
- **Afterstory:** The high-score table in every later arcade has the player's initials in second place. First place is always "THX".

### 15. The Weather Station
*(Pseudo Stage, Mystery Episode)*

- **Cold open:** A mountain weather station with four doors. Snow is coming out of one and heat-haze out of another.
- **The episode:** The forecaster is missing. Her Castform is still here and is beside itself: each room of the station is stuck in a different weather, and it changes form at every doorway. The player explores four rooms with Castform as a guide. Each room's puzzle is solved by the right weather-form; Castform cannot choose its form, so the player must lead it through rooms in the right order.
- **Endings:** The forecaster is in the instrument tower, where she has been trying to stop a storm she believes she caused. She did not. With Castform's four forms in the right order the storm is read, not stopped, and passes.
- **Reward:** The Castform asks to see real weather: it joins with **Forecast Ahead**: at the start of each battle the player is told which weather, if any, the opponent's team can set.
- **Afterstory:** A hand-written forecast is pinned up at the start of each later stage. It is right four times in five.

### 16. The Thing in the Well
*(Mystery Episode, Miniature Episode)*

- **Cold open:** A village has stopped using its well. A bucket comes up by itself, full of small, carefully stacked pebbles.
- **The episode:** Something down there is leaving gifts and the village is terrified of it. The player can lower a light, lower a Pokémon, lower themselves, or lower a gift in return. It is a Feebas, washed in by a flood years ago, too ugly to have ever been picked up, and it has been trying to make friends with the only thing it ever sees: the bucket.
- **Endings:** Send a gift down and it sends back something better each time (three exchanges). Lift it out: it hides under the player's coat from the villagers. Tell the village the truth: half of them are ashamed and half do not believe it.
- **Reward:** The Feebas, with **Unlooked-For**: opponents' accuracy against it is lowered by 10% while it is at full HP. If it ever evolves, the villagers who did not believe it are the ones who come to look.
- **Afterstory:** The well is in use again in the Final Reunion. There is a pebble on the rim.

## C. Communities and world events

### 17. The Stranding
*(World Event, Community Episode)*

- **Cold open:** A whole beach town is running in one direction. On the sand: a Wailord, tide going out.
- **The episode:** Six tasks, eight turns, and the player has only so many Pokémon: keep it wet (Water moves), shade it (anything large or winged), dig a channel (Ground, Fighting), calm it (Sing, or any Pokémon it can see eye to eye), hold the crowd back, and guide the rising tide. Each party Pokémon can do one task per turn. Townspeople cover whatever the player leaves, less well.
- **Endings:** Four or more tasks kept up to the eighth turn: it floats. Fewer: a second Wailord arrives offshore and calls, and the first one drags itself the last metres; it lives, hurt. There is no ending in which it dies.
- **Reward:** A Wailmer that came in with the tide follows the party's boat. **Gratitude** (a region effect, not an ability): the next sea crossing in this region skips one node, because something very large is giving the player a lift.
- **Afterstory:** The town holds a festival on the anniversary in every later run of this save. The player's Pokémon that kept the Wailord calm is on the banner.

### 18. The Day the Center Closed
*(Community Episode, minigame)*

- **Cold open:** The Pokémon Center's doors are locked from inside. Through the glass, a Chansey is trying to run the front desk, the phones and the healing machine at once.
- **The episode:** The nurse has collapsed from overwork. The player takes a shift: a triage game across six patients, with party Pokémon assigned as helpers (a Fire-type warms, a Psychic-type calms, a strong one carries). Mistakes make the queue longer, not worse.
- **Endings:** A clean shift: the nurse wakes to find everything done and cries a little. A messy one: she wakes, sighs, and fixes it in two minutes, and thanks the player just as much.
- **Reward:** Because healing items are no longer used in battle, this matters: **Standing Welcome**. For the rest of the region, the first heal at any Center also restores the party's revival item by one, up to its normal cap.
- **Afterstory:** The Chansey sends a parcel to the player's next region. It is mostly bandages. One of them has a drawing on it.

### 19. The Snowbound Village
*(Pseudo Stage, World Event)*

- **Cold open:** An avalanche has taken the only road. A Delibird lands in front of the player with an empty sack and a note: *medicine, by nightfall.*
- **The episode:** A sled run in four legs with a Stantler team, across a frozen lake, through a pine wood, over a pass and down. The player chooses how much to carry: every extra crate slows the sled and makes one wild encounter harder, and every crate left behind is a house in the village that goes without.
- **Endings:** Counted in crates delivered, out of five. Five is very hard. Three is a good day. The village does not blame anyone for the two.
- **Reward:** By crates: warm clothes (the party ignores hail and snow damage this region), a Delibird that insists on coming, and at five, the lead Stantler's bell, a held item: **Herd Bell**: allies switching in take no damage from entry hazards the turn after the holder leaves.
- **Afterstory:** If any crate was left behind, a child from that house finds the player two regions later, grown, and well, and says so.

### 20. The Drought Ranch
*(Community Episode, moral choice)*

- **Cold open:** A rancher is pouring the last of a water tank into a trough. Twelve Miltank. Water for nine.
- **The episode:** The spring upstream has been dammed by a Bibarel colony that is also short of water. The player can break the dam (a battle), move the colony (an escort), dig a second channel (a Ground or Water Pokémon and two nodes of time), or walk away.
- **Endings:** Break it: the ranch is saved and the colony scatters; three Bidoof are seen on the road, thin. Move it: slow, and everyone lives. Dig: the best result and the most expensive in time. The rancher never asks the player to choose the first one. She only stands there.
- **Reward:** Breaking it pays the most money. Digging pays in **Fresh Milk** at every rest in this region (an out-of-battle full heal for one Pokémon, once per rest).
- **Afterstory:** In a later region a Bibarel is building something by a road. If the colony was scattered, it stops and watches the player go by.

### 21. The Orchestra
*(Community Episode, rhythm minigame)*

- **Cold open:** Dusk in a meadow. Forty Kricketot tune up and then all stop at once and look at an empty tree stump.
- **The episode:** The conductor, an old Kricketune, has lost its nerve after a bad concert. The player can conduct in its place (a rhythm game: four sections, entering one at a time), or find the conductor and talk it round by playing its own recording back to it.
- **Endings:** The player conducts well: the old Kricketune comes out of the grass at the last movement and takes over, and the player gets the best seat. The player conducts badly: the orchestra covers for them, loudly. Bringing the conductor back first skips the game and earns the same reward with a different last line.
- **Reward:** A young Kricketot from the back row, with the move **Encore Performance**: a status move; the user repeats its own last move next turn with +1 priority. It fails if used twice in a row.
- **Afterstory:** The orchestra is heard, faintly, at the start of every night node for the rest of the region.

### 22. The Alley Court
*(Community Episode)*

- **Cold open:** A Meowth drops a coin in front of the player, on purpose, and runs. It is leading them somewhere.
- **The episode:** The city's strays have a court, on a rooftop, under a Persian who has ruled for years by being the best thief. A younger Meowth is on trial for *sharing*. The strays want an outsider to judge, because nobody else is neutral.
- **Endings:** Rule for the Persian: order holds, and the player is paid in stolen goods. Rule for the Meowth: a challenge follows, cat against cat, and the player may coach the Meowth through it. Refuse to rule and ask what the kittens think: they vote, badly, and it works.
- **Reward:** The young Meowth joins, with **Fair Share**: when it picks up or steals an item in battle, its ally in a Double Battle (or the next Pokémon sent in) heals 10%.
- **Afterstory:** Coins appear on windowsills along the player's route for the rest of the region. The strays are paying a debt they have decided they owe.

### 23. The Appliance Shop
*(Hub Destination, Miniature Episode)*

- **Cold open:** A repair shop where the fan, the fridge, the oven, the washing machine and the lawnmower are all arguing.
- **The episode:** One Rotom has been moving between five appliances because the old repairman talks to each of them differently, and it likes all five conversations. The repairman is retiring. It has to pick one, or none.
- **Endings:** The player can help it choose by using each appliance for an errand around town (five tiny tasks). It settles on the one whose errand it enjoyed, or leaves the shop as a plain Rotom and takes the repairman's radio.
- **Reward:** A Rotom in the chosen form. If it left plain: **House Call**: once per battle it may change to any appliance form on entry, and stays that form until it faints.
- **Afterstory:** The shop is a hub in later runs: one free held-item repair per visit.

### 24. The Night Shift at the Day-Care
*(Interrupting Episode)*

- **Cold open:** The day-care couple are standing in their garden at midnight holding a frying pan and a broom.
- **The episode:** Egg thieves have hit three day-cares this week. The player is asked to stand watch. Five short waves through the night, each from a different side of the garden; between waves the player can place two party Pokémon as sentries. The thieves are not a team. They are four hungry Sneasel and a Weavile, and the eggs are the only food they know how to find.
- **Endings:** Drive them off (five wins). Or, after the second wave, leave food at the fence: the raids stop, and the Weavile comes to look at the player through the gate. Or lose a wave: one egg is taken and found again next stage, hatched, and being looked after by the Sneasel.
- **Reward:** An egg of the player's choice from three. If the food was left: a Sneasel that stays to guard the garden, and can be collected on any later visit.
- **Afterstory:** A hatchling raised by Sneasel has a nature no day-care would give it.

### 25. The Mural
*(Region-Wide Story, Community Episode)*

- **Cold open:** A Smeargle is painting on a long blank wall at the edge of the first town. It has drawn one figure: the player, arriving.
- **The episode:** It paints one panel per stage. The panels are built from what the player actually did: the Pokémon they caught, the fight they nearly lost, the event they walked past. The player never sees it paint. They only find a new panel when they pass a wall.
- **Endings:** At the region's end the mural is whole and can be walked along. Things the player is not proud of are in it too, painted without comment.
- **Reward:** The Smeargle offers to join. Its first **Sketch** may copy any move that was used *against* the player during this region.
- **Afterstory:** In the Final Reunion there is one wall per region. Players who stayed out of an evil team's climax find that panel left blank.

## D. Rivals, duels and tournaments

### 26. The Rival's Missing Ace
*(Rival Episode)*

- **Cold open:** The rival is in the road, turning out their bag, not pretending to be fine.
- **The episode:** Their ace is gone: not stolen, it walked off in the night after a loss. The rival asks for help, in whatever way their personality allows (some cannot ask, and stand there until the player offers). A search across three nodes, with the rival alongside. What they say while searching is the most the player will ever learn about them.
- **Endings:** The ace is found practising alone, ashamed of the loss. The rival talks to it; the player may stay or step away. Stepping away raises Trust the most. If the player refuses to help, the rival finds it alone, later, and remembers who was not there.
- **Reward:** No item. Relationship: Trust +2 and a memory flag that changes the rival's lines for the rest of the run. Their ace remembers the player's Pokémon by sight.
- **Afterstory:** In the rival's next battle with the player, the ace is sent out first, and bows.

### 27. The Little Cup
*(Tournament Episode)*

- **Cold open:** A banner: *NO EVOLVED POKÉMON. NO EXCEPTIONS. YES, WE CHECK.*
- **The episode:** A sixteen-entrant knockout for first-stage Pokémon only, all set to the same level. The player enters three. Opponents are children, grandparents, one very serious man with a clipboard, and the previous champion: a girl with a Sunkern that has never lost and nobody knows why.
- **Endings:** Win it, or lose to the Sunkern, which turns out to know exactly one trick and to do it perfectly. If one of the player's entrants is ready to evolve mid-tournament, the player must choose: cancel the evolution and stay in, or evolve and withdraw.
- **Reward:** The **Little Cup** (held item): a first-stage Pokémon holding it has its defenses raised by 30%. A Pokémon that cancelled its evolution to stay in gains Bond +2.
- **Afterstory:** The Sunkern's trainer writes to ask how the player beat it, or to explain, kindly, how they could have.

### 28. The Ring Under the Bridge
*(Tournament Episode, evil-team adjacent)*

- **Cold open:** Shouting under a road bridge. A Scrafty with one eye swollen shut is being sent out again.
- **The episode:** An illegal fight ring. The fighters are not volunteers. The player can enter (three fights for money, with the bookmaker hinting which one to lose), sit in the crowd and learn who runs it, or open the cages behind the ring during the main bout.
- **Endings:** Win all three honestly: the ring cannot pay, and offers the Scrafty instead. Throw the fight as told: good money, and the Scrafty watches the player do it. Open the cages: chaos, a chase, and every fighter free; the ring's owner turns up again later as an evil-team recruiter with a grudge.
- **Reward:** The Scrafty, with **Rope-a-Dope**: each time it is hit by a contact move, its next attack gains +10% power, to a cap of +20%.
- **Afterstory:** A freed fighter is met in a later region, behind the counter of a dojo, teaching children to block.

### 29. The Borrowed Team
*(Rival Episode, challenge)*

- **Cold open:** A referee holds out two hats.
- **The episode:** An exhibition where the player and the rival **swap teams** for one battle. The player commands the rival's Pokémon, with their moves and their habits; the rival commands the player's. Each side's Pokémon obey, and are visibly not sure about it.
- **Endings:** Whoever wins, both learn something: the player sees the rival's full team and movesets for the rest of the run, and the rival sees the player's. Afterwards one of the rival's Pokémon lingers by the player, and one of the player's lingers by the rival.
- **Reward:** Winner: a prize item. Both: the Pokémon that lingered gains a one-battle bonus the next time the two trainers meet.
- **Afterstory:** The rival's later dialogue about the player's Pokémon becomes specific. They have held the reins.

### 30. The Cheer Squad
*(Tournament Episode, Companion Story)*

- **Cold open:** A Plusle and a Minun are cheering for both sides of a street battle, impartially, with pom-poms made of leaves.
- **The episode:** They attach themselves to the player for a stage and cheer in every battle from the sideline: a small announced effect each turn (one cheers attack, one cheers defense). They argue about which matters more. At the stage's end each asks, separately, to be told it was the important one.
- **Endings:** Pick one: it joins; the other waves from the road, and is cheering for the rival next time. Pick both: both join. Tell them the truth, that it only worked because there were two: both join, and stop arguing for a full day.
- **Reward:** One or both. Together they have **Call and Response**: in a Double Battle, when one uses a status move the other's next move gains +1 priority. Once per turn.
- **Afterstory:** If they were separated, the Final Reunion is where they find each other.

## E. Legends, promises and long roads

### 31. The Fox at the Shrine
*(Region-Wide Story, Legendary-adjacent)*

- **Cold open:** A roadside shrine, nine stone lanterns, one lit. A Ninetales watches from the roof.
- **The episode:** It has guarded a promise for a very long time and has forgotten who made it. It asks nine small things of the player across the region, one per lantern: carry water, return a lost thing, forgive a debt, keep silent for a node, lose something on purpose. None is a battle. Some cost something.
- **Endings:** Each task done lights a lantern. At nine, the Ninetales remembers: the promise was made to it, by a trainer who said they would come back. The player is not that trainer. The Ninetales decides the promise has been kept anyway.
- **Reward:** By lanterns. At five: a charm that halves curse and burn damage. At nine: a Vulpix kit it has been raising, with **Thousand-Year Promise**: the first time each battle that an ally would faint, it is left at 1 HP instead. Once per battle.
- **Afterstory:** Refuse a task and that lantern stays dark in every later run on this save, until someone lights it.

### 32. The Storm Chaser
*(Legendary Episode, Region-Wide Story)*

- **Cold open:** Lightning hits the same hill three times. Something is standing on it.
- **The episode:** A Raikou is crossing the region ahead of the weather. It is never a random battle. It appears at the edge of three storms in three stages, and each time it is doing something: drinking, watching a town, standing over a Pichu that got too close. How the player behaves at each sighting (approach, wait, throw a Ball, shelter the Pichu) is remembered.
- **Endings:** A player who only ever threw Balls meets it a fourth time as a battle, fairly, and it runs if it is losing. A player who waited each time meets it a fourth time at a rest, where it walks through the camp, looks at each Pokémon, and leaves a scorch mark shaped like a paw.
- **Reward:** The battle path can catch it. The patient path gives the **Thundermark**: with it, Raikou's Legendary Blessing (if the player has it) gains two points of Influence, and Electric-types in the party act first on the first turn of any battle fought in rain.
- **Afterstory:** The Pichu, if sheltered, is met later as a Pikachu that is not afraid of thunder.

### 33. The Old Courier
*(Reunion Episode, Pseudo Stage)*

- **Cold open:** A post office is holding a small party. The guest of honour is a Dragonite with grey around its eyes and a mailbag it will not take off.
- **The episode:** It has flown the mountain route for forty years. Today is its last day and there is one parcel left, addressed to a lighthouse, and a storm between here and there. It asks the player to come: it is not sure of its wings. A flight in three legs; the player's Pokémon ride along and take the wild battles so that it can keep flying.
- **Endings:** Delivered: the lighthouse keeper is the child of the first person it ever delivered to. If the storm forces it down, the player's Pokémon carry the parcel the last mile and it walks beside them.
- **Reward:** A Dratini egg from the postmaster ("it's his grand-something"), which hatches knowing *Extreme Speed* and has **Tailwind Heart**: on entry, the next ally to switch in this battle gains +1 Speed. Once per battle.
- **Afterstory:** A retired Dragonite is seen asleep on post office roofs in later regions. Parcels for the player arrive a node early.

### 34. The Warning
*(World Event, Mystery Episode)*

- **Cold open:** A village is throwing stones at an Absol on the ridge. It does not leave.
- **The episode:** It turns up before disasters, and the village has drawn the wrong conclusion. The player can drive it off (the villagers pay for this), stand between it and the stones, or follow it. It leads to a crack in the hillside above the school.
- **Endings:** If the player believed it, there is time: an evacuation in six turns, with party Pokémon carrying, digging and shouting. If the player drove it off, the slide comes at night; the Absol is already there, digging at the school door, and the player can still help, with three turns instead of six. Nobody dies in either version. Buildings do.
- **Reward:** The Absol does not join anyone who threw a stone. Otherwise it may, with **Before the Fall**: at the start of each battle the player is told the opponent's single strongest move, and the first critical hit against the Absol's side each battle is an ordinary hit.
- **Afterstory:** The village builds it a small shrine. In a later run the stones are still on the ridge, in a neat pile. Somebody stacked them.

### 35. The Storyteller's Road
*(Region-Wide Story, Mystery Episode)*

- **Cold open:** An old woman at a rest node is telling a story to children, about a trainer. The trainer has the player's starter.
- **The episode:** She is at one rest per stage, always a little ahead, always mid-story, and the story is always about what the player is *about to do*. It is wrong in small ways. If the player acts to make it right, or acts to prove it wrong, she adjusts, and looks pleased either way.
- **Endings:** At the region's end the player may ask who she is. She gives three answers and says two are lies. Or the player may tell *her* a story, built from five choices; the children vote on whether it is better.
- **Reward:** If the children prefer the player's story: a Slowking that has been sitting at the back the whole time gets up and follows, with **Twice-Told**: the first move used against it in each battle is 20% weaker the second time it is used.
- **Afterstory:** In the next region a different old woman is telling the player's story, wrongly.

### 36. The Lantern Sea
*(Hub Destination, Community Episode)*

- **Cold open:** At night the bay lights up from below.
- **The episode:** Once a year the Chinchou and Lanturn of the deep come up to the shallows and the town floats paper boats out to them, one for each person who did not come home. A walkable night hub: boat-makers, a diver who trades pearls for stories, a child who has lost her boat and is looking for it in the reeds, and a Lanturn that has swum up to the jetty and is waiting for one boat in particular.
- **Endings:** The player may float a boat of their own, for a Pokémon lost or released in any run. If they do, the Lanturn at the jetty follows it out.
- **Reward:** A Chinchou that liked the player's boat, with **Guiding Light**: accuracy cannot be lowered for its side while it is on the field.
- **Afterstory:** Every later visit, there is one more boat than the town can account for.

### 37. The Second Nest
*(Miniature Episode, moral choice)*

- **Cold open:** A Fletchinder is feeding a nest. The chick in it is twice its size and the wrong shape.
- **The episode:** It is raising a foundling: a Rufflet whose egg rolled into the wrong tree. The flock wants it gone before it grows talons. A wild Braviary has been circling for days and has not come down. The player can drive off the flock, carry the chick to the Braviary, or do nothing and watch what the Fletchinder does.
- **Endings:** If the player does nothing, the Fletchinder stands in front of the nest against its own flock and loses, and the Braviary finally comes down. The chick goes with the Braviary and looks back. The Fletchinder is left on the branch.
- **Reward:** Whoever is left alone at the end may join: the Fletchinder, with **Foster Wing**: when an ally with less than half HP switches out, this Pokémon switches in with a +1 Defense boost. Once per battle.
- **Afterstory:** A Braviary with a habit of sitting on low branches, like a much smaller bird, appears in a later region.

### 38. The Long Table
*(Finale Episode, Community Episode; pairs with your Final Reunion)*

- **Cold open:** On the night before the last battle of the run, there is a table in a field, far too long, and it is being laid.
- **The episode:** Everyone the player helped in the run brings a dish. There is a place setting for each event that ended well, an empty chair for each that did not, and a small pile of stones at the end for anything the player walked past. The player can sit anywhere, and who they sit beside decides which two short conversations they get. No battle. No reward screen.
- **Endings:** The table is cleared at dawn. Whatever was on the player's plate (a berry from the ranch, a malasada, a pebble from a well) is in the bag in the morning.
- **Reward:** For each filled place: +1% to the whole party's stats in the final battle, to a cap of 10%. The number is shown, with the names beside it.
- **Afterstory:** This is the afterstory.
